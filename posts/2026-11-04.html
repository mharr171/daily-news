General news

Top story

Xavier Becerra is elected California’s next governor

Democrat Xavier Becerra, a former state attorney general, was called the winner as soon as polls closed, beating Republican Steve Hilton, a British-born commentator, in the race to succeed term-limited Gov. Gavin Newsom. Bloomberg described him as California’s first Latino governor in 150 years.

Why it matters: The next governor will write the state budget and sign the bills that fund transit, schools and health care, and will lead California’s response to federal pressure over elections.

Developing

Prop. 40 billionaire tax trails and voter ID Prop. 39 is rejected

Prop. 40, a one-time tax on billionaires’ net worth to pay for health care and education, was trailing with about 44% yes and 56% no in early returns, and CalMatters reported it appeared headed for defeat. Prop. 42, which would ban new wealth taxes, led with 55% on Tuesday night, while the Associated Press called Prop. 39, the voter ID measure, as rejected. Counting continues, and the numbers can still shift.

Why it matters: The result decides whether California adds a new tax on its richest residents, which opponents had spent more than $118 million fighting, and whether voters will have to show ID at the polls.

Developing

Measure V is passing in San Mateo County, and two Millbrae council races are close

In results updated at 12:05 a.m., Measure V, the community college bond, had about 64% yes and needs 55% to pass. In Millbrae, Sissy Riley ran unopposed in District 2, Ann Schneider led Juan Gámez 337 to 306 in the District 3 short-term race, and Albert Yam led Bob Nguyen 497 to 421 in District 4. County measures also led comfortably: J (filling supervisor vacancies) about 74% yes, L (weather resilience) about 69%, U (redistricting) about 81% and AA (civil rights) about 72%. The county will keep counting, and mail ballots postmarked by Tuesday can still arrive through Nov. 10.

Why it matters: Schneider leads by 31 votes and Yam by 76 so far, so late-counted ballots could still change who sits on the Millbrae City Council.

Bloomberg: Bay Area voters approve the regional transit sales tax

Bloomberg reports that voters passed the regional sales tax meant to fund the Bay Area’s trains and buses, calling it a financial lifeline for BART and the region’s other struggling systems. Agencies including Caltrain, Muni and AC Transit had warned of deep service cuts if it failed, and backers had raised $7.3 million. County-by-county totals were not available in the reporting, and counting continues.

Why it matters: If the result holds, Caltrain and BART riders on the Peninsula avoid the service cuts the agencies had planned for.

Players arrive by boat at the Ferry Building around noon, and fans can start gathering at 9 a.m.; the parade runs north along the Embarcadero to Second Street. What happened to Pope Leo XIV’s lung surgery? He leaves Friday on a 12-day South America trip

Three weeks after the pope announced his surgery, doctors removed the lower lobe of his right lung on Oct. 13, and the Vatican says the disease was localized and no further treatment is needed. On Monday a Vatican spokesperson said the 71-year-old is in good health and needs no extra precautions, with his usual medical staff and ambulances traveling as on earlier trips. He leaves Friday for Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, ending Nov. 17: about 19,000 miles, 10 cities and 36 speeches, including a stop in Cusco at about 11,000 feet, where oxygen may be needed.

Why it matters: The first American pope has a large following among Bay Area Catholics, including parishes across San Mateo County.

Good news

Beavers are about to return to Loch Ness after 500 years

Scotland’s nature agency has licensed two nonprofits to release up to 15 beaver families over three years in the River Ness area, which includes Loch Ness, and releases could begin within weeks. Beavers were last recorded there in 1526–1527. The animals build ponds and wetlands that other species use and can help soften floods and droughts, and more than 73% of people who responded to the consultation supported the plan.

Good News Network (citing NatureScot), Nov 3, 2026

U.S. politics & government

Top story

Democrats win control of the House and the Senate

Democrats won both chambers, ending a Republican majority that was 53–47 in the Senate and 219–214 in the House entering the night, and NBC News projects Democrats’ largest House majority in nearly two decades. In the Senate, NBC counted Democratic flips in North Carolina (Roy Cooper), South Carolina (Annie Andrews), Texas (James Talarico over Ken Paxton) and Ohio (Sherrod Brown), while The Guardian’s live blog counted as many as seven, including Kansas, Iowa and Maine. Alaska’s Senate race was still being counted, and tallies may change.

Why it matters: Democrats will now control which bills reach the president and how Congress oversees federal spending, including aid for Bay Area transit, health care and disaster response.

Vance blames the Iran war for Republican losses, and Trump says he will work with Democrats

Vice President JD Vance said in a Wednesday post that the Iran war was the reason Republicans lost, Axios reported. Trump told Fox News the war was partly responsible, according to France’s Le Figaro, and was reported to say he would accept the results and work with Democrats; on election night he posted that he was not on the ballot and would have won in a landslide. The White House has also pushed a message that Trump remains the president.

Why it matters: The war has pushed up fuel prices, with California gas still $1.25 above a year ago, and a Democratic Congress can now hold hearings and set terms on war spending.

Democrats win governorships in Texas, Georgia and New Mexico, and add Senate firsts

Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor’s race, Keisha Lance Bottoms won in Georgia and will be the first Black woman to serve as a governor, and Deb Haaland won in New Mexico as the first Native American woman to hold the office; Republican Byron Donalds won Florida. In the Senate, Chris Pappas of New Hampshire becomes the first openly gay man elected there and Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota the first Native American woman, while Michigan’s Abdul El-Sayed was projected to win and would be the first Muslim senator. A record 17 states have now elected female governors.

Democrats lead in key California House seats as Richard Pan unseats Kevin Kiley

The South China Morning Post reports Democrat Richard Pan defeated incumbent Republican Kevin Kiley, helping the next Congress include a record six lawmakers of Chinese descent. Al Jazeera reported Democrats leading in the Central Valley’s 22nd District, where Randy Villegas was about 30 points ahead of incumbent David Valadao, and near San Diego, though the AP had not called those races. In the Bay Area, Scott Wiener led Connie Chan by about 10 points for Nancy Pelosi’s seat with half the vote counted.

Why it matters: Wiener, who would succeed Pelosi, is poised to represent San Francisco in the House that now controls the agenda.

MLB offers to delay its salary cap to 2028 as a lockout looms

Baseball’s owners proposed postponing a salary cap until 2028, with the cap set at $258.2 million, up from the first offer of $245.3 million, and a floor of $180.2 million. The players’ union is expected to reject any plan that includes a cap, and the sport is heading toward a lockout in December. For the Giants, who are starting a pivotal off-season, the dispute could freeze or slow free-agent and trade business.

Science & tech

Voters back limits on data centers, from governors’ races to town ballots

Data Center Watch reviewed the public positions of 74 candidates in the 36 governor’s races: 43% backed a ban or moratorium on new projects, 51% backed more regulation, and about 5% took no position. In Texas, Democrat Gina Hinojosa defeated Gov. Greg Abbott, who had paused data center development pending energy and water audits earlier this year. Of 46 towns with local restriction measures on the ballot, 43 had approved them when the article was published.

The Register, Nov 4, 2026

Hackers target French blood supplies after the supermarket freezer attacks

Attackers reached a maintenance supplier’s systems for the French blood service and tried to push plasma freezer temperatures from about −22°F up to 230°F, according to a French report, and France’s health ministry says the change was caught and corrected quickly with no serious consequences. The attempt came days after a second round of hacks on European supermarket freezers and two weeks after France’s cybersecurity chief warned that refrigeration systems could be targeted. Britain’s National Cyber Security Centre chief called the moment a “Sliding Doors” turning point and warned of a sharp rise in incidents.

TechRadar, Nov 4, 2026

NASA tests drone-based landing navigation meant for the Moon

NASA’s SPLICE system, which maps terrain and diverts to a safer site if a landing spot looks unsafe, flew on an Alta-X drone at Rogers Dry Lake near Edwards, California, completing simulated lunar descents on Aug. 27. The agency says the tests raised the technology’s readiness level and plans to list the guidance software in its public catalog this fall, and several commercial lunar lander companies are already working with versions of it. A full-scale test in Mojave is planned later this month.

NASA, Nov 4, 2026

Hubble finds dwarf galaxies are missing most of their oxygen

Using Hubble to study 37 nearby dwarf galaxies, including NGC 4449, the Galaxies Losing Oxygen via Winds (GLOW) project found that these small systems keep only a small share of the oxygen their stars make. The project’s name points to galactic winds carrying it away, though NASA’s image page does not confirm that mechanism. NASA released the image Nov. 2.

NASA, Nov 4, 2026
