Issue No. 21

Wednesday, October 21, 2026

San Mateo County’s district attorney says he won’t criminally charge ousted Sheriff Christina Corpus, and a new CNN poll puts President Trump’s approval at a record low two weeks before the midterms. The 49ers are 6–0 after Monday’s win over Washington as the Warriors open their season tonight in Los Angeles, and SpaceX says it will try to catch both halves of Starship on its next flight.

In this edition:

General news

DA won’t file criminal charges against ousted Sheriff Christina Corpus

San Mateo County District Attorney Steve Wagstaffe has closed his criminal investigation of former Sheriff Christina Corpus and her former chief of staff, finding no crime even though he called the 2024 arrest of a deputy union leader unjustified. Wagstaffe still plans to pursue four civil grand jury accusations against Corpus, which could bar her from holding elected office again. Supervisors removed her from office a year ago.

Why it matters: The decision shapes the last chapter of a scandal that cost county taxpayers an undisclosed sum in investigations and legal fees.

Palo Alto Daily Post, Oct 19, 2026

Trump’s approval hits a record low in CNN polling as Democrats lead the generic ballot

A CNN/SSRS poll taken Oct. 15–18 found 32% of Americans approve of President Trump’s job performance, the lowest in CNN’s polling, with approval among Republicans at 74%. Registered voters favor Democrats 49% to 39% on the generic congressional ballot. The Nov. 3 midterms are less than two weeks away.

Why it matters: Several competitive California House races on the Nov. 3 ballot will help decide which party controls Congress.

CNN (via KESQ), Oct 21, 2026

California touts its first new steel mill in 50 years, powered by solar

Governor Newsom highlighted Pacific Steel Group’s Mojave Micro Mill in Kern County, which will melt scrap steel in an electric furnace run by an on-site solar farm and batteries. The company has committed more than $540 million and expects about 700 jobs, aided by a $30 million state tax credit. The mill is set to open in 2027 and make about 380,000 tons of rebar a year.

Why it matters: Locally made rebar could feed California construction and infrastructure projects, including Bay Area housing and transit work.

Office of the Governor, Oct 19, 2026

SF supervisors seek emergency legal aid funding after an ICE detention in SoMa

A coalition of San Francisco supervisors and immigrant legal groups rallied at City Hall on Tuesday for emergency money for legal services and the city’s rapid-response network. The call followed Monday’s detention of a man in South of Market by masked ICE officers, captured on video, in which he shouted that he is a U.S. citizen. No dollar amount or vote has been set.

Why it matters: Immigrant families across the Peninsula rely on San Francisco’s legal aid network when enforcement actions happen nearby.

CBS News Bay Area, Oct 20, 2026

Sports

Today in sports: 49ers improve to 6–0; Warriors open at the Lakers tonight

The 49ers beat Washington 23–17 on Monday Night Football at Levi’s Stadium behind two Christian McCaffrey touchdowns and a George Kittle scoring catch, with Brock Purdy throwing for 264 yards; they visit Atlanta on Sunday. The Warriors open their season tonight at the Lakers, and the Earthquakes, already playoff-bound, play at FC Dallas on Saturday. In the WNBA Finals, the Valkyries lost Game 2 to Atlanta 79–65 on Monday, tying the series 1–1 before Game 3 tonight in Atlanta.

Fox Sports, Oct 20, 2026

Giants weigh free-agent arms after a 97-loss season

With four young starters recovering from Tommy John surgery and a staff ERA that ranked 23rd, the Giants are expected to shop for pitching this winter, and Buster Posey has called the bullpen a priority. MLB.com lists closers David Bednar and Raisel Iglesias and starters Michael King and former Giant Kevin Gausman among possible targets. Free agency opens after the World Series.

MLB.com, Oct 20, 2026

The Terminal List returns and Slow Horses wraps season 6 today

Prime Video drops all eight episodes of The Terminal List season 2, and Apple TV airs the Slow Horses season 6 finale along with the season 2 premiere of Where’s Wanda?. Disney+ adds the live-action Moana after its summer theatrical run. Tonight ABC airs a two-hour CMA tribute to Dolly Parton that benefits her Imagination Library.

TVLine, Oct 21, 2026

Jon Stewart extends his Daily Show deal through the 2028 election

Skydance Television said Monday that Jon Stewart will keep hosting The Daily Show on Comedy Central through December 2028 and remain an executive producer. He will continue hosting on Mondays while the news team rotates through the rest of the week. Stewart returned to the show in 2024 after first hosting from 1999 to 2015.

TVLine, Oct 19, 2026

Science & tech

SpaceX will try to catch both Starship stages on its next test flight

Elon Musk said on SpaceX’s quarterly earnings call that Starship Flight 15, expected in November, will attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and, for the first time, the Ship upper stage with the launch tower’s arms. Success would mean an orbital rocket fully recovered for reuse. SpaceX also reported $14.6 billion in third-quarter revenue.

Space.com, Oct 20, 2026

Japan launches MMX to bring back samples from Mars’ moon Phobos

JAXA’s Martian Moons eXploration probe lifted off on an H3 rocket from Tanegashima on Monday and is due at Mars in August 2027. It plans to land on Phobos twice, carry a small French-German rover, and return a sample to Earth in 2031. NASA contributed instruments and will receive part of the sample.

Space.com, Oct 19, 2026

ChatGPT adds a way to lock private chats behind a PIN or Face ID

OpenAI began rolling out Locked Chats on Tuesday for most ChatGPT plans, including Free, letting users hide chosen conversations behind a four-digit PIN or Face ID or Touch ID. Locked chats move to their own section and stay out of search and regular memory. Resetting the PIN permanently deletes them, and the feature doesn’t add encryption.

TechCrunch, Oct 20, 2026

Federal agencies ordered to remove DeepSeek AI from their systems

The Department of Homeland Security directed federal civilian agencies to stop buying DeepSeek products and remove them from existing systems, including third-party tools built on its models. DHS cited the risk that Chinese authorities could access government data. Agencies can request exceptions, and China’s Foreign Ministry objected to the move.

TechCrunch, Oct 20, 2026
