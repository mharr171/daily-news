General news

Top story

San Mateo County still has about 100,000 ballots to count, and Millbrae’s District 3 race is down to a few votes

The county’s last report, as of Friday afternoon, counted 179,539 ballots and left about 100,000 unprocessed, nearly all of them mail ballots; the next update is due by 4:30 p.m. today. Millbrae’s semi-official figures from the same day show Juan Gámez ahead of Ann Schneider 484 to 481 in District 3 and Albert Yam leading Bob Nguyen 700 to 586 in District 4. The city says none of it is final until the county certifies, and its next update is Thursday; voters with 853 challenged ballots can fix them until Nov. 25.

Why it matters: A three-vote margin means every remaining ballot, and every challenged ballot that gets fixed, could decide who represents District 3.

Your local government this week

Your local government this week: Millbrae’s council takes up the Monterey Street linear park and paid parking on Tuesday

The City Council meets Tuesday at 7 p.m. in Council Chambers and on Zoom. The agenda has no public hearings; the main items are a status update and vote on the concept design for the Monterey Street Linear Park Improvements Project, a summary of feedback from the Oct. 22 open house on the Millbrae Paid Parking Program, and resolutions accepting the Aviador Avenue Bridge Shoring and Lomita Avenue Slope Stability projects as complete, plus Veterans Day and Native American Heritage Month proclamations. No Planning Commission meeting is posted for this week, and City Hall is closed Wednesday for Veterans Day.

Why it matters: The linear park design and the parking program feedback shape how downtown streets look and what it costs to park in Millbrae.

U.S. politics & government

Top story

Trump tells “60 Minutes” his deportation push may have been “too tough”

In a CBS interview, President Trump said his immigration enforcement went too far in cases involving long-time workers whose only violation was entering illegally, and that he would like to work with Democrats on the issue. He also said he may make “maybe a few” cabinet changes, still backs Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and is not looking to escalate the Iran war. Exit polls cited by CBS found 58% of voters said his immigration actions went too far.

Why it matters: California’s large immigrant workforce is directly affected by how hard enforcement stays, and a bipartisan immigration deal would be felt in Bay Area farms, restaurants and construction.

CBS News, Nov 8, 2026

Democrats are poised to reject Trump’s $1.5 trillion defense request as the Dec. 11 funding deadline looms

Bloomberg reports the Democratic majorities are expected to turn down the request, a 44% jump, and might approve only about $1 trillion, a figure it attributes to one unnamed person. A planned $350 billion add-on also looks doomed. Current stopgap funding runs out Dec. 11, and The Hill says another continuing resolution is the likeliest outcome.

Why it matters: Stopgap funding or a shutdown affects federal workers, defense and tech contractors, and federal grants that flow to California.

Bloomberg (via Yahoo Finance), Nov 8, 2026; The Hill, Nov 8, 2026

Hegseth faces impeachment talk as Jeffries calls him a “hard yes”

Bloomberg reports House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said he is a “hard yes” on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, on a measure that could be introduced as soon as this week. Earlier coverage by NPR and The Hill had him calling for Hegseth to go without endorsing impeachment, and Republican Rep. Thomas Massie has said he will bring articles unless Hegseth resigns. No vote is scheduled, and Trump told “60 Minutes” he backs Hegseth.

Why it matters: The Iran war and Pentagon leadership bear on Bay Area defense contractors and military families at Travis Air Force Base.

Bloomberg, Nov 8, 2026; The Hill, Nov 8, 2026

Congress returns this week with Republican leadership votes and a permitting-bill test in the Senate

House Republicans choose their new leaders Tuesday after Speaker Mike Johnson said he won’t seek the minority leader job, with Steve Scalise and Jim Jordan as the contenders, The Hill reports. The Senate holds a procedural vote Monday on a permitting-reform bill, and the House has passed only three of the 12 annual spending bills.

Why it matters: Who leads the minority party, and how the funding fight goes, set the terms for California priorities such as wildfire funding and water.

The Hill (via Yahoo News), Nov 8, 2026

Weekend recap: 49ers rout the Raiders, Warriors win a seventh straight, and the Quakes and Hawaiʻi play Saturday
