Issue No. 1

Thursday, October 1, 2026

Gov. Gavin Newsom closed out his final bill-signing season with new limits on addictive social media features for teens, a child marriage ban and a slate of AI rules, while forecasters say Bay Area warmth could last into mid-October. Nationally, the Supreme Court let third-country deportations continue and the Pentagon confirmed the last U.S. troops have left Iraq. The unbeaten 49ers get ready for Denver, and SpaceX’s Crew-13 is set to launch this morning.

In this edition:

General news

Newsom wraps up his last bill-signing season as governor

Gov. Gavin Newsom signed and vetoed his last bills as governor by Wednesday’s deadline, working through a final batch of 1,160 measures and nearly 8,000 over his time in office. New laws include a 13-bill youth online safety package that bars social media companies from offering addictive features such as infinite scroll to users under 16, a ban on child marriage, stronger state antitrust powers, tax credits for local news outlets and a ban on public officials issuing crypto meme coins.

Why it matters: Peninsula families with teens will see new limits on how social apps can hook kids, and struggling local newsrooms that cover San Mateo County could tap the new tax credits.

CalMatters, Oct 1, 2026

Bay Area warm spell could last into mid-October

Above-normal temperatures that arrived Tuesday are expected to hang on, with the 70s in San Francisco and the mid-80s to 90s inland, and a National Weather Service forecaster said the warmth could run into the middle of October. The coast should stay in the 60s and low 70s, and fire danger will be elevated, though strong winds aren’t in the forecast. San Francisco could see its first October streak of 10 straight 70-degree days since 2015.

Why it matters: Bayside and inland Peninsula neighborhoods should expect warm afternoons for days: drink water, check on heat-sensitive neighbors and avoid anything that could spark a fire.

KQED, Sep 29, 2026

Supreme Court lets third-country deportations resume for now

On Tuesday the Supreme Court allowed the Trump administration to keep deporting people to countries other than their own while the justices decide whether migrants must first get a meaningful chance to object. The order pauses an appeals court ruling that federal attorneys said had forced the cancellation of a flight carrying about 70 people. The administration has sent about 25,000 people to more than two dozen countries, most of them to Mexico.

Why it matters: San Mateo County is home to many immigrant families, and relatives facing removal could be sent to countries where they have no ties while the case plays out.

PBS News, Sep 29, 2026

Last U.S. troops leave Iraq, ending the anti-ISIS mission there

The Pentagon said the last U.S. troops deployed for the fight against ISIS have left Iraq, departing Erbil Air Base in the Kurdistan region with their equipment. The task force has moved to Jordan, hundreds of U.S. troops remain in Syria, and the U.S. will keep offering training and intelligence support. Kurdish officials warned the pullout leaves their region exposed to Iranian strikes and a possible ISIS comeback.

Why it matters: It closes a chapter that began in 2003 and touched many California military families, while U.S. forces in Jordan and Syria stay close to tensions with Iran.

Al Jazeera, Sep 30, 2026

Sports

Today in sports: unbeaten 49ers turn to Denver

The 49ers are 3-0 and alone atop the NFC West after Sunday’s 36-30 win over Arizona, when Brock Purdy threw for 297 yards and four touchdowns; Denver visits Levi’s Stadium this Sunday at 1:25 p.m. on CBS. The Warriors opened training camp at BYU–Hawaii in Laie this week ahead of their preseason opener against the Clippers on Sunday in Honolulu. The Earthquakes beat Portland 3-1 on Saturday and next play Oct. 10, while the Giants’ season is over.

NBC Sports (ProFootballTalk), Sep 27, 2026

Giants stand by Vitello after a 97-loss season

Buster Posey gave manager Tony Vitello a vote of confidence after a first season that ended with 97 losses and the Giants missing the playoffs for the ninth time in 10 years. Posey took the blame for a bullpen that blew 27 saves and said adding dependable relievers is the top offseason priority; the team also expects Randy Rodríguez back from Tommy John surgery next season.

MLB.com, Sep 30, 2026

Culture

Tom Cruise’s Digger splits critics ahead of Friday’s opening

Alejandro G. Iñárritu’s oil-industry satire, starring Tom Cruise as tycoon Digger Rockwell alongside John Goodman and Riz Ahmed, sat at 53% on Rotten Tomatoes as reviews rolled in. BBC Culture praised it as an art-house spectacle, while IndieWire likened it to a big-budget Dr. Strangelove riff that feels cut off from real human truth. It opens in theaters Friday.

NME, Sep 30, 2026

Early Verity review calls the Colleen Hoover thriller a twisty, fun ride

Michael Showalter’s adaptation of Colleen Hoover’s best-seller stars Dakota Johnson as a broke writer hired to ghostwrite a famous author’s series, and Anne Hathaway as the injured novelist whose remote Vermont home hides a disturbing confession. Time Out gave it three of five stars, calling it a mostly fun ride that still piles on one twist too many. It opens in theaters Friday.

Time Out, Sep 30, 2026

Science & tech

SpaceX Crew-13 set to launch to the space station this morning

NASA astronauts Jessica Watkins (commander) and Luke Delaney (pilot), Canada’s Joshua Kutryk and Russia’s Sergey Teteryatnikov are scheduled to lift off at 11:10 a.m. EDT (8:10 a.m. Pacific) on a Falcon 9 from Cape Canaveral. Forecasters gave a 55% chance of acceptable weather, with scattered showers possible, and NASA will stream the launch and docking.

NASA, Sep 30, 2026

California adds new AI rules for workplaces, health care and deepfakes

Newsom signed 13 AI bills on Wednesday: employers can’t rely only on AI to discipline or fire workers, must disclose when AI drives a mass layoff, and can’t put surveillance tools in workplace bathrooms. Other laws protect doctors’ judgment when AI tools are used in care, bar stripping digital watermarks from AI content, and require gene synthesis companies to screen orders, including Peninsula legislators’ bills SB 1000 (Sen. Josh Becker) and AB 1864 (Asm. Marc Berman).

Office of the Governor, Sep 30, 2026
