General news

California sues to keep the National Archives in San Bruno

Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a federal lawsuit Wednesday to stop the National Archives from closing its San Bruno facility, which holds more than 75,000 cubic feet of federal records from Northern California and the Pacific region. The suit says the agency skipped required public and tribal consultation; the records, including immigration, court and Japanese American incarceration files, would be moved to sites in Riverside, Colorado, Maryland, Texas and Missouri. Rep. Kevin Mullin has introduced a bill requiring congressional approval before such closures.

Why it matters: The archive sits next door to Millbrae and is a key resource for Peninsula families tracing immigration and family records, fewer than 4% of which are digitized.

Justice Department sues California to set aside 20,000 mailed ballots

The U.S. Justice Department sued California and Los Angeles County election officials on Thursday, asking a federal court to set aside, and block the counting of, mail ballots sent to more than 20,000 voters it says are likely noncitizens. Secretary of State Shirley Weber said the state has not seen data backing the claim and will not disenfranchise voters on that basis, and legal experts note similar federal suits have failed. Ballots are already out statewide ahead of the Nov. 3 election.

Why it matters: Nothing has changed yet for San Mateo County voters, but the case could affect how ballots are handled statewide in the final weeks before Election Day.

Construction begins on 501 apartments at Redwood City’s Elco Yards

High Street Residential has started building two apartment buildings with 501 units at Elco Yards in Redwood City, with completion expected in 2029. The El Camino Real building will include about 5,300 square feet of ground-floor retail. The groundbreaking follows a 2025 City Council vote that reduced the number of units reserved for lower-income households, a change the developer said it needed to make the project financially feasible.

Why it matters: New homes on the El Camino corridor add supply in a county with steep rents, though fewer will be priced for low-income households than first planned.

Redwood City Pulse, Oct 16, 2026

California gas averages $6.26 as the Iran war drives prices up nationwide

California drivers are paying an average of $6.26 a gallon, far above the $4.33 national average, after Iranian attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz sent oil prices soaring. U.S. prices have risen $1.35 a gallon since the war began, and the national average hit a record in September. Democrats are now running ads blaming Republicans for the spike, while Republicans point to California’s gas tax and climate rules.

Why it matters: Higher pump prices hit Peninsula commuters directly and are becoming a central issue in California’s competitive House races.

Falcon 9 launches a classified Space Force payload from Vandenberg

A SpaceX Falcon 9 lifted off from Vandenberg Space Force Base on the Central Coast at 5:01 p.m. Thursday carrying a classified payload for the U.S. Space Force on the USSF-481 mission. No details about the satellite’s purpose were released.

CHP holds driver, not Tesla, responsible after sleeping motorist on Full Self-Driving hits patrol car

The CHP says a driver using Tesla’s Full Self-Driving system told officers he was asleep when his car struck a patrol car on I-80 in Fairfield late Tuesday, seriously injuring two officers who have since been released from the hospital. The agency stressed that FSD is a driver-assistance system that requires constant supervision and is holding the driver responsible. Investigators will pull the car’s data and may ask the DMV to revoke his license.
