General news

Top story

San Mateo County has about 50,000 ballots left to count, and the next update is Thursday

The county’s latest report, as of Monday at 4 p.m., shows 232,443 ballots processed, 52,904 of them since Friday’s report, with an estimated 50,000 still to go: about 42,500 mail ballots that arrived by Election Day, 5,800 that arrived afterward, and 1,700 conditional voter registrations. The next update is due Thursday by 9:30 a.m., and the county certifies on Dec. 3. Millbrae’s council races are still on the city’s Nov. 6 numbers, with Juan Gámez ahead of Ann Schneider by three votes in District 3; the city’s next update is also Thursday. About 1,037 challenged ballots can be fixed until 5 p.m. on Nov. 25.

Why it matters: With a three-vote gap in Millbrae’s District 3 race and 50,000 county ballots still uncounted, the outcome could change before certification.

Gas and grocery watch

Gas and grocery watch: California gas falls to $5.74, still about $1.04 above a year ago

AAA’s Monday figures put California’s regular-gas average at $5.74, down from $5.91 a week earlier and $6.33 a month ago, but well above the $4.71 of a year ago. The San Francisco metro area averages $5.78, and the national average is $4.01. Oil has also slid about $20 a barrel since late October on hopes of a U.S.–Iran peace deal, which could keep pump prices easing. AAA doesn’t list San Mateo County separately, and I didn’t find a new food-price report today.

Why it matters: Cheaper gas helps Peninsula commuters, but prices are still a dollar higher than last year.

Rep. Massie pushes to force a House vote to impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth, and Trump hits back

Kentucky Republican Thomas Massie introduced eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and spoke about forcing a vote on Monday. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries has said he is a “hard yes” on removing Hegseth, and Arizona Democrat Greg Stanton called Hegseth’s tenure “a disaster.” Later Monday, in a surprise appearance in the White House briefing room, President Trump called Massie a “stone cold loser.”

Why it matters: The fight over the Pentagon’s leadership comes as Congress returns to deal with the Dec. 11 funding deadline, which affects defense contractors and federal workers across California.
