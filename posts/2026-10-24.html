Saturday, October 24, 2026

Bay Area voters are weighing a regional transit tax that decides the future of BART and Caltrain service, and San Francisco declared a local emergency to get ready for a wet El Niño winter. Abroad, burning tankers near the Strait of Hormuz kept US–Iran tensions high. The unbeaten 49ers head to Atlanta, DC’s Clayface opens in theaters, and NASA laid out a faster path back to the Moon.

In this edition:

General news

Tankers burn near Hormuz as US–Iran tensions rise before the midterms

Iran says seven oil tankers were set on fire or damaged in and around the Strait of Hormuz, and its Revolutionary Guard warned that new US strikes could bring a wider response. Analysts see a US attack before the Nov. 3 midterms as unlikely, but rhetoric on both sides keeps rising. Brent crude traded around $114 a barrel, up from about $72 before the war began in February.

Why it matters: Oil near $114 keeps gas prices high for Bay Area drivers and adds to the cost of everything shipped in.

Al Jazeera, Oct 23, 2026

Bay Area transit tax on the Nov. 3 ballot would decide BART and Caltrain cuts

The Regional Transit Measure would add a half-cent sales tax in San Mateo, Alameda, Contra Costa, and Santa Clara counties and a one-cent tax in San Francisco for 14 years starting in April 2027, raising about $980 million a year for BART, Caltrain, Muni, and others. It needs a simple majority across the five counties. If it fails, BART could start closing low-ridership stations as early as January, and Caltrain faces major cuts.

Why it matters: Millbrae’s BART and Caltrain station is a major hub, so the vote shapes both your sales tax and how often trains run on the Peninsula.

Pleasanton Weekly, Oct 22, 2026

Measure U would take San Mateo County’s district maps away from the supervisors

Measure U would create an independent commission to draw the county’s five supervisor districts after each census, ending the board’s final say. Supporters point to 2021, when the board passed over a citizen panel’s maps and kept lines close to the old ones. No argument against it was filed, and ballots are due Nov. 3.

Why it matters: District lines decide which supervisor represents Millbrae and how its voice is weighed on county issues.

The Almanac, Oct 23, 2026

San Francisco declares a local emergency to prepare for El Niño storms

Mayor Daniel Lurie declared a local emergency so city departments, including SFO, Muni, and Public Works, can buy flood barriers, pumps, and generators faster ahead of a possibly record-breaking El Niño winter. The city is preparing for coastal flooding, power outages, and damage to public buildings. Public Works offers up to 10 free sandbags per address.

Why it matters: The same storms will hit the Peninsula, and SFO is on the list of agencies getting ready, so delays and outages are worth planning for now.

SFist, Oct 23, 2026

Millbrae tests payment options as downtown paid parking takes shape

The city’s downtown parking plan would add paid parking on the busiest blocks and could expand residential permits within a quarter mile of them. Residents tried out payment options at drop-in sessions on Thursday. Rates, hours, zones, and equipment are still undecided and will go to future City Council meetings, and a garage on one of three city lots is only being studied.

Why it matters: Paid spaces downtown would change what errands and dining cost, and the council meetings ahead are the place to weigh in.

City of Millbrae, Oct 22, 2026

American Express to cut about 3,500 jobs despite a 28% jump in profit

The card company will trim roughly 5% of its workforce, its first large round of cuts in about a decade, even after third-quarter profit rose to $3.71 billion. Leadership called it an efficiency push, not a response to weaker business. It did not say which teams or locations will be affected.

Why it matters: Even profitable companies are trimming staff, a sign of a cooler job market for Bay Area office workers.

Associated Press via KPRC Click2Houston, Oct 23, 2026

Newsom’s office says California homicides fell to their lowest level since 1966

Visiting San Quentin for its film festival, Gov. Gavin Newsom’s office reported that homicides fell 18.6% from 2024 to 2025, with robbery down about 20% and car theft down about 26%. It also cited lower reoffending rates for people who complete rehabilitation programs and announced new funding for prison education and job training. The figures come from the governor’s office.

Why it matters: Statewide drops in car theft and robbery tend to show up in local numbers too, which matters for Peninsula drivers and shop owners.

Office of the Governor, Oct 23, 2026

NASA lays out a faster Artemis plan aimed at a 2028 Moon landing

At Kennedy Space Center, NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and agency leaders outlined how they plan to speed up Artemis toward a crewed landing in 2028. Under the plan, Artemis III sends four astronauts to low Earth orbit next year to test systems needed for landing, and Artemis IV becomes the first mission to touch down.

NASA, Oct 23, 2026

Anthropic opens Claude Duet, an AI agent that works while your app is closed

Anthropic began inviting US Pro and Max subscribers from a waitlist to Claude Duet, a research preview that scans email and calendar each morning and keeps working in its own cloud environment. It pauses for approval before payments, signatures, or deletions, and it shares the plan’s usage limits.

VKTR, Oct 22, 2026

Meta pauses Muse chat sharing after thousands of shared links were found online

Researchers found more than 6,000 shared Meta Muse conversations because each link carried the chat’s title, and some held loan applications and internal business reports. Meta switched off sharing while it fixes how links can be discovered. The lesson for any AI tool: share a summary instead of the full chat, and review links you’ve already shared.
