A high surf warning brings 25- to 30-foot breakers to the Bay Area coast this weekend

The National Weather Service has issued a high surf warning for the Bay Area coastline from 6 p.m. Friday until 4 a.m. Sunday, with dangerous breaking waves of 25 to 30 feet expected. Officials are asking people to stay out of the ocean and off beaches, jetties, piers and rocks, warning that one slip could be deadly and that rescues put first responders at risk. It comes days after a woman was swept off a beach in Santa Cruz County during unusually high tides.

Why it matters: Coastside beaches from Pacifica to Half Moon Bay will be dangerous all weekend, so plan walks and outings away from the water’s edge.

Capitola Beach is closed after nine children are sickened in an E. coli outbreak

Santa Cruz County Public Health closed Capitola Beach on Tuesday after nine children from Santa Cruz County and other Bay Area counties were infected with E. coli O157, with officials linking the cases to likely exposure at the beach. The source is still under investigation, and no reopening date has been set; families are asked to keep children and pets out of the water and away from untreated creek and lagoon water. Separately, San Luis Obispo County health officials confirmed one death in an E. coli outbreak tied to a pumpkin patch that closed before Halloween.

Why it matters: Some of the sick children are from Bay Area counties, so Peninsula families who visited Capitola recently should watch for symptoms, which usually start 3 to 10 days after exposure.

Two killed and 17 hurt when a Brooklyn building collapses beside a construction site

A three-story building on Flatbush Avenue fell Wednesday afternoon into a neighboring lot where excavation was underway, killing two people and injuring 17, including seven children; four are in critical condition. More than 250 firefighters and paramedics responded, and the fire commissioner said the search could last several days. City building inspectors and the Brooklyn district attorney are looking into whether the construction work played a role.

Why it matters: Collapses near excavations raise the same questions about inspections and neighbor protections that come up with infill construction on the Peninsula.

A maritime academy cadet pulls a father and son from a Foster City pool and restarts the son’s breathing

A Cal Poly Maritime Academy cadet heard screaming near his home in Foster City, climbed a pool fence and pulled out a father floating face down, then found the man’s 25-year-old son unconscious at the deep end. He brought the son out too and gave CPR until he began breathing on his own, before Foster City police and San Mateo Consolidated Fire arrived. The son was conscious when paramedics took him to the hospital, and the cadet received a lifesaving commendation from Foster City police.

Democrat Scott Colom wins Mississippi’s Senate race, the party’s eighth flipped seat

CBS News projects that Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, has defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in a race that was separated by a few dozen votes the day after the election before his lead grew. It is Democrats’ eighth Senate pickup this year, after North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas and Maine, giving them at least 55 seats to Republicans’ 44 in January, with Alaska still undecided. Mississippi had not elected a Democrat to the Senate in more than 40 years.

Why it matters: A larger Democratic Senate majority will shape confirmations, the budget and federal policy toward California starting in January.

USDA proposes tighter SNAP rules that it estimates would drop 6.3 million people

The proposal, released Tuesday, would narrow “broad-based categorical eligibility,” so households over the income limit could no longer qualify just because they receive non-cash help such as child care or job training. The department’s own estimate is that 6.3 million people would lose food aid in 2029, when the rule would take effect, and about 1.8 million children would lose free school lunches. The White House defended the change; the top Democrat on the House Agriculture Committee called it heartless. The rule is open for public comment.

Why it matters: California runs CalFresh under the same federal rules, so the change could reach working families in San Mateo County who now qualify through those programs.

An Army sentinel at Arlington National Cemetery scolded a group that included Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth after someone laughed following a photo, CBS News reports; it is unclear who laughed or whether Hegseth made any noise. Why it matters: Scrutiny of the Pentagon’s leadership is growing as Congress heads toward the Dec. 11 funding deadline, which covers bases and contracts in California.

United will block AI agents from booking flights for travelers

United Airlines said it will bar unauthorized third-party AI agents from making reservations, citing the security risk of handing bots logins and payment details and federal rules such as in-person bag checks. It is updating its booking policies, though fares will still show up on sites such as Google Flights, Kayak and Hopper; Southwest, by contrast, lets ChatGPT users see its flights.

CBS News, Nov 11, 2026

Parker Solar Probe finds the solar wind’s magnetic twists flip direction at small scales

Researchers using data from NASA’s Parker Solar Probe saw, for the first time directly, that turbulent magnetic fields in the solar wind rotate one way at large scales and the opposite way at small ones, with the switch growing stronger farther from the Sun. The reversal happens where the plasma stops behaving like a fluid, and the team says it could help forecast geomagnetic storms that disrupt satellites and power grids.
