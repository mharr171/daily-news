Issue No. 28

Wednesday, October 28, 2026

Federal officials gave California counties 48 hours to set aside ballots tied to a disputed list of suspected noncitizens, and local registrars are pushing back. An SF Standard review traced about $164 million through Mayor Daniel Lurie’s political and charity network, and the high-speed rail board votes today on its first trains. The Rays stayed alive in the World Series with a 12-inning win, and a possible new dwarf planet turned up beyond Neptune.

In this edition:

General news

Trump administration presses California counties over a disputed noncitizen voter list

Federal officials asked nearly every California county to agree within 48 hours to set aside ballots from people on a list of suspected noncitizens, and warned of possible criminal charges. Registrars in Butte, Fresno and San Benito counties say early checks found many listed people weren’t registered or receiving ballots. State officials dispute the data and have refused to comply.

Why it matters: San Mateo County’s elections office is among those facing the federal demand days before ballots are counted.

CalMatters, Oct 27, 2026

About $164 million has flowed through Mayor Lurie’s political and charity network

An SF Standard review found roughly $163.7 million has moved through political, philanthropic and personal channels tied to Mayor Daniel Lurie since 2023, most of it charitable. Lurie has reported $53.9 million in donations made at his request in under two years, and his four November ballot measures have raised about $18.6 million. Opposition campaigns have raised far less.

Why it matters: San Francisco’s Nov. 3 ballot measures and homelessness programs shape the city many Peninsula residents commute to and visit.

The San Francisco Standard, Oct 28, 2026

California high-speed rail board votes today on its first train order

The board is scheduled to consider a $772.1 million contract with Siemens Mobility for three trainsets, with options for 19 more and 30 years of maintenance. The first trains would arrive in late 2029, with service between Merced and Bakersfield targeted for 2033–34. On Thursday the board votes on the final environmental review for the Los Angeles-to-Anaheim segment.

Why it matters: The planned route would eventually run up the Peninsula on Caltrain’s tracks to Millbrae, so each step in the Central Valley sets that timeline.

Streetsblog California, Oct 26, 2026

Most California Republicans in Congress haven’t signed a pledge to seat election winners

A bipartisan pledge by Reps. Don Bacon and Josh Gottheimer commits signers to seat every member-elect with a valid certificate after the midterms. Of California’s Republican-aligned members, only Rep. Kevin Kiley, now an independent, signed; others declined to say or didn’t respond. Republicans hold a narrow House majority, so a few votes could matter.

Why it matters: Several competitive House races in California could help decide control of Congress next year.

CalMatters, Oct 27, 2026

Today in sports: Rays survive in 12; Warriors and Quakes play tonight

Tampa Bay beat Milwaukee 4–3 in 12 innings on Tuesday to cut the Brewers’ World Series lead to 3–1, with Game 5 tonight at Tropicana Field. The 1–3 Warriors play at Minnesota tonight after their win in Denver, and the Earthquakes host Colorado in their second-to-last regular-season match. The 49ers are on their bye before hosting the Raiders on Nov. 8.

Science & tech

Astronomers spot a possible new dwarf planet beyond Neptune

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile found an object called 2026 ED138, about 46 times Earth’s distance from the Sun. It could be roughly 600 to 1,500 kilometers across, large enough to be a dwarf planet. Astronomers want infrared measurements, likely from the James Webb Space Telescope, to pin down its size.

Space.com, Oct 28, 2026

GitHub Copilot Fusion now picks the best AI model for each request

Copilot Fusion is now available on all Copilot plans in VS Code, JetBrains IDEs, the Copilot app and the CLI. Instead of choosing one model, users pick an effort level, and Fusion routes each task to one model or has models draft, check and revise together. Paid plans get 30% off model costs with Fusion for a limited time.

GitHub, Oct 28, 2026

Astroscale pitches ‘bodyguard’ spacecraft to protect satellites

The space cleanup company wants to adapt its debris-removal technology into escort craft that would shield valuable satellites from hostile approaches. Its defense chief says one could be ready to launch in under three years at about 1% of the cost of a military satellite network. The pitch follows recent close passes by Russian satellites; no contracts are signed yet.
