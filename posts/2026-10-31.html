Saturday, October 31, 2026

Job cuts kept coming as October ended, including small rounds at Dropbox and Front in San Francisco. Tech companies are now among the biggest outside spenders in California’s legislative races. The Warriors evened their record with a win over the Lakers, and tonight’s sky offers Saturn and early Halloween fireballs.

In this edition:

General news

Layoffs pile up as October ends, including cuts in San Francisco

Companies announcing cuts this week included Comcast, Amazon’s devices unit, Disney’s theme parks, and Alaska Air Group, which plans about 200 non-contract job cuts including 48 in Hawaii. In San Francisco, state notices list 57 jobs at Dropbox and 53 at Front, and a Levy operation in Oakland will close Dec. 31 with 181 workers affected.

Why it matters: Bay Area tech cuts are small but steady, a sign of a tougher local job market heading into the holidays.

JobAdvisor roundup, Oct 30, 2026

Tech money makes up at least a fifth of outside spending in California legislative races

Spending on Assembly and Senate races topped $128 million as of Oct. 29, up from $74 million in 2022, and groups funded by Google, Meta, and other tech interests account for at least 20% of contributions to outside spenders. Unlike candidate donations, which are capped at $5,900, these independent expenditures have no limit.

Why it matters: Outside money is shaping the people who will write California’s rules on AI, data centers, and tech jobs that matter to Peninsula workers.

Science & tech

Halloween night sky: Saturn and early fireballs

Around 7 p.m., look east-southeast for Saturn, a bright yellow-white point about two fists above the horizon; a small telescope shows its nearly edge-on rings. Early Taurid meteors, the so-called Halloween fireballs, can streak from the east-northeast, with the shower peaking in the second week of November.

Space.com, Oct 31, 2026

China’s secretive space plane is visible before dawn this week

Shenlong, China’s reusable space plane on its fourth mission, can be seen as a steady moving point of light in the predawn sky. In California, a good pass is expected over Los Angeles early on Nov. 2; Bay Area skywatchers can find local times on Heavens-Above.

OpenAI releases GPT-6 Rosalind for life sciences research

OpenAI released GPT-6 Rosalind, a model built for biological reasoning and scientific tool use. Access is limited to approved organizations through the company’s trusted-access program for research.
