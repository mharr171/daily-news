General news

Top story

Southbound U.S. 101 through San Francisco is closed until Monday morning, the last closure of the repaving project

Caltrans closed all southbound lanes of U.S. 101 between 17th and Cesar Chavez streets, near the Hospital Curve at Zuckerberg San Francisco General Hospital, at 11 p.m. Friday. Lanes are due to reopen by 6 a.m. Monday, Nov. 9; northbound 101 stays open. Caltrans recommends southbound I-280 as the detour, and the Fourth, Seventh and 10th Street on-ramps and the westbound I-80 connector to northbound 101 are blocked. A Caltrans spokesperson said this is the final 101 closure for the repaving work, which earlier shut northbound lanes in September, southbound lanes in October and I-80 on Oct. 23–26.

Why it matters: Peninsula drivers headed into San Francisco or back south on Saturday and Sunday will want to plan for I-280, which is the posted detour.

KQED, Nov 5, 2026

Developing

Regional transit tax: about 61% yes, mailed ballots still being counted, and the tax starts April 1

Richmond Confidential reports that transit agencies called victory on election night with “yes” at about 61% of ballots counted so far, and that mailed ballots will be counted until Nov. 10, so the results are preliminary. The measure would raise about $980 million a year for 14 years through a half-cent sales tax in Alameda, Contra Costa, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties and a full cent in San Francisco, with the money going to 27 transit agencies and taking effect April 1. A judge rejected a lawsuit over the ballot wording. Supporters have said the alternative was deep service cuts and the possible closure of 10 BART stations.

Why it matters: The money decides whether Caltrain and BART service on the Peninsula is kept or cut, and it adds to what San Mateo County shoppers pay in sales tax from April.

Good news

A venomous primate thought extinct for 100 years turns out to be alive on an Indonesian island

Scientists had treated the Bangka slow loris as possibly extinct, with no documented Western sighting since the 1930s. A new paper by Austrian and international researchers uses records from a rescue center on Bangka Island, which rescued more than 90 of the animals between 2015 and 2025 and released 86 of them, to show the species still exists and is probably more widespread on the island than assumed.

U.S. politics & government

Top story

Justice Department drops its bid to hold back about 20,000 disputed California ballots

The department asked a federal judge on Friday to dismiss its own lawsuit, which sought to keep California from counting ballots it said belonged to noncitizens until their status could be checked. California officials said the list was out of date and included legitimate voters, and the judge had denied the federal request. The department said it will keep pursuing suspected election-law violations through other cases, including a separate lawsuit over California’s voter rolls. State officials said about 9 million ballots had been processed by early Friday, with nearly 5 million still to count.

Why it matters: All of California’s ballots will now be counted as normal, so close local races, including Millbrae’s council seats, can keep shifting as the remaining ballots come in.

Democrats say ending the Iran war is a first priority, while Trump keeps threatening more strikes

Rep. Adam Smith of Washington, expected to chair the House Armed Services Committee, said ending the war will be one of the first things Democrats do, and that he expects a bill cutting off further war funding that Trump would likely veto, according to Common Dreams. The Pentagon puts the cost above $47 billion, and the article cites an NBC News exit poll in which 63% of voters called launching the war the wrong decision. Trump said he would rather not destroy the rest of the country but “maybe I’ll have to,” and Rep. Thomas Massie, a Republican, plans to force a vote next week on impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The next government funding deadline is Dec. 11.

Why it matters: War costs and a December funding fight affect federal spending that reaches California, and fuel prices tied to the conflict have already raised costs for Bay Area transit.

Meta buys Conductor, a tool for running several AI coding agents at once; its cloud service shuts down Nov. 20

Conductor lets a developer run multiple coding agents, including Claude Code, Codex and Cursor, in separate copies of a codebase and review their work. Axios first reported the deal on Nov. 4 and Conductor confirmed it is joining Meta; terms were not disclosed. The hosted cloud version turns read-only Nov. 13 and shuts down Nov. 20, when stored cloud data is deleted, while the Mac app keeps working until early 2027, and active subscribers will get refunds.
