Issue No. 27

Tuesday, October 27, 2026

High tides flooded bayside roads in Marin on Monday, and a coastal flood advisory runs through Thursday for low spots around the bay. With a week to go, California’s mail ballots must be postmarked by Election Day, and Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 workers. The Warriors got their first win in Denver, and Star Trek’s Walter Koenig has died at 90.

In this edition:

General news

King tides flood bayside roads, with an advisory through Thursday

High tides swamped parts of Shoreline Highway, Mill Valley and a Highway 101 off-ramp near Sausalito around midday Monday, stranding at least one driver. The National Weather Service has a coastal flood advisory for low-lying shoreline areas until 3 p.m. Thursday, with the San Francisco gauge expected about 2 feet above normal near midday today. Forecasters say tide timing can vary by up to 90 minutes around the bay.

Why it matters: Low bayside roads and trails along the Peninsula shoreline can flood around midday high tides this week, so allow extra time near the water.

CBS San Francisco, Oct 26, 2026

A week to Election Day: California mail ballots must be postmarked by Nov. 3

Mail ballots count if they are postmarked on or before Election Day and arrive within seven days, but officials urge voters to send them early. Voters can also drop off or cast ballots at vote centers now open in every county, and track their ballot online. The ballot includes the governor’s race between Xavier Becerra and Steve Hilton and 14 statewide propositions.

Why it matters: San Mateo County voters can mail, drop off, or vote at one of 10 county vote centers through Nov. 3.

CalMatters, Oct 27, 2026

A river otter turns up on the San Mateo coast for the first time in over a century

Wildlife cameras at Midpen’s Cloverdale Ranch preserve near Pescadero photographed a river otter in July, August and September. River otters haven’t been known on the San Mateo coast since the 1800s, after fur trapping and pollution wiped them out. Biologists say the return signals clean water and healthy habitat, and the River Otter Ecology Project asks the public to report sightings.

Why it matters: It’s a sign that protected land on the county’s coast is recovering, the same streams that support local salmon and steelhead.

Bay City News via Redwood City Pulse, Oct 27, 2026

Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees

Microsoft announced Monday that roughly a quarter of its workforce, at level 70 and below on participating teams in the US, Canada, India and the UK, may take a voluntary exit with standard severance. Employees have until early December to decide and would leave by year-end. The company says layoffs remain possible.

Why it matters: Microsoft employs many Bay Area tech workers, and a buyout this large signals more belt-tightening across the industry.

The Deep Dive, Oct 27, 2026

Walter Koenig, Star Trek’s Chekov, dies at 90

Walter Koenig, who played Ensign Pavel Chekov in the original Star Trek and its first seven films, died Sunday at his North Hollywood home. He debuted in the 1967 episode “Amok Time,” appeared in 36 episodes, and later played Alfred Bester on Babylon 5. He also wrote two memoirs and an episode of the animated Star Trek.

Science & tech

OpenAI releases GPT-6.1 Terra for writing and computer use

OpenAI added GPT-6.1 Terra to its API on Monday, describing it as a fast, efficient model for visual reasoning, precise computer use and natural writing. Developers can use it through the Responses and Chat Completions endpoints. It is aimed at the kind of quick drafting and on-screen tasks that apps built on OpenAI’s models handle for everyday users.

OpenAI, Oct 26, 2026

NASA ends the Swift observatory’s mission after a rescue attempt fails

NASA formally ended Swift, the gamma-ray burst observatory launched in 2004, after a private spacecraft built to boost its sinking orbit malfunctioned. Swift has no thrusters and is expected to fall back to Earth in the next few weeks. Designed for two years, it worked for more than 20 and observed over 2,000 gamma-ray bursts.

Space.com, Oct 26, 2026
