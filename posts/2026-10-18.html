General news

AC Transit details deep cuts if the Bay Area transit tax fails

The Regional Transit Measure on the Nov. 3 ballot would add a half-cent sales tax in San Mateo, Alameda, Contra Costa and Santa Clara counties and a one-cent tax in San Francisco, raising about $1 billion a year for 14 years for agencies including BART and Caltrain. AC Transit says that without it, it would cut service 16%, pull 100 buses and lay off up to 300 workers starting in June 2027. Its board expects to consider a final contingency plan on Dec. 9.

Why it matters: San Mateo County voters help decide this measure, which would fund the BART and Caltrain service Millbrae riders rely on.

KQED, Oct 16, 2026

Rumors of a statewide ICE operation spread as Central Valley arrests climb

Advocates say more than 100 farmworkers and others have been detained in the Central Valley this week, while unverified reports of a 45-day operation targeting San Francisco, Los Angeles and San Diego have spread; Bay Area rapid-response groups say they have not seen a local surge. Gov. Gavin Newsom urged residents to know their rights. A state law bars arrests within 200 feet of polling places on Election Day, though it does not cover early voting.

Why it matters: Peninsula immigrant families and advocates are preparing for possible enforcement, and the polling-place protections matter as voting gets underway.

KQED, Oct 16, 2026

San José says it’s ready for a strong El Niño winter

Federal forecasters give an 83% chance of a historic El Niño from October through December, and San José says upgrades since the 2017 Coyote Creek flood, funded partly by voter-approved Measure T, leave it far better prepared. Valley Water has built more than 8,000 feet of flood walls along Coyote Creek and recently awarded a $100 million contract for the next phase. The City Council will get an update on its highest-risk flood sites in the coming weeks.

Why it matters: The same storm pattern threatens the Peninsula, so this is a good time to clear gutters and storm drains before the rains arrive.

KQED, Oct 16, 2026

Unruly passenger forces a Singapore-bound flight back to SFO

Singapore Airlines flight SQ31 returned to SFO on Wednesday night, about nine hours after departing, after the airline said a passenger became unruly and injured himself. Passengers got off while the plane refueled, and it left for Singapore around 11:20 p.m. with a new crew. Social media claims of an arrest have not been confirmed by the airline.

Why it matters: Turnbacks like this can leave SFO travelers rebooking long-haul connections, a reminder to keep airline alerts on for overnight flights.

Quakes clinch first playoff berth since 2023 with draw against Nashville

Preston Judd’s 70th-minute goal, set up by Eduard Löwen, answered Hany Mukhtar’s first-half strike as San Jose tied league leader Nashville 1-1 at PayPal Park on Saturday and clinched a place in the MLS Cup Playoffs. The Quakes (13-8-9, 48 points) sit fourth in the West and are unbeaten in nine league matches. They visit FC Dallas next Saturday at 7:30 p.m.

Nick Bosa and Deebo Samuel ruled out for Monday night against Washington

The 49ers ruled out defensive lineman Nick Bosa (knee, calf), receiver Deebo Samuel Sr. (hamstring), linebacker Garret Wallow and safety Marques Sigle for Monday’s game against Washington. Receivers Mike Evans (ribs, Achilles) and Christian Kirk (calf) are questionable, while fullback Kyle Juszczyk is expected back after missing Week 5. Final decisions come 90 minutes before kickoff at Levi’s Stadium.

Science & tech

Blue Origin plans more Florida launch pads to speed up New Glenn flights

Blue Origin says it will build more Space Coast launch pads modeled on its Cape Canaveral complex, add two landing ships and spend $330 million expanding its Rocket Park factory to build up to 150 upper stages a year. The company is still recovering from a May test-fire explosion and has asked the FAA to raise its permitted launches at LC-36A from 12 to 50 a year; a virtual public hearing is set for Oct. 22.

Week in review

General news: Stolen ballots and a federal fight over California votes

San Mateo County urged voters to check their ballots after mail stolen from a Daly City post office included an unknown number of them, and the Justice Department sued to set aside mail ballots sent to more than 20,000 Californians it calls likely noncitizens. Prosecutors charged a former 49ers executive and a contractor in an alleged $1.4 million kickback scheme on San Mateo County Event Center projects, and California sued to keep the National Archives facility in San Bruno open.
