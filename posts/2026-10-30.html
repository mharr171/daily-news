Issue No. 30

Friday, October 30, 2026

The head of California’s high-speed rail agency quit amid a travel expense scandal, and federal prosecutors subpoenaed California election workers despite a judge’s warning. Coastal flooding is possible along Bay Area shorelines through Sunday. The Dream forced a Game 7 in the WNBA Finals, Patrick Stewart died at 86, and astronauts marked the 100th U.S. spacewalk from the space station.

In this edition:

General news

California high-speed rail CEO resigns amid a travel expense scandal

Ian Choudri stepped down as CEO of the California High-Speed Rail Authority late Wednesday, effective immediately, weeks after the board limited his contracting power over a report that consultants misspent $592,000 on travel. Finance Director Joe Stephenshaw is expected to serve as interim chief. The project’s budget now stands at $129 billion with a 2040 target, and the board was set to vote Thursday on buying Siemens trains.

Why it matters: High-speed rail is planned to reach San Francisco along the Caltrain corridor through Millbrae, so turmoil at the agency shapes long-term rail plans on the Peninsula.

The San Francisco Standard, Oct 29, 2026

Federal subpoenas hit California election workers despite a judge’s warning

At least four California election workers in Los Angeles and Orange counties have received federal grand jury subpoenas seeking evidence of noncitizen voting, a day after Judge David O. Carter wrote that threats against election staff “must stop.” Three Los Angeles County employees are due before a grand jury on Nov. 4. The Justice Department says a subpoena doesn’t make someone a target; critics call the timing an effort to intimidate officials during the election.

Why it matters: County election offices, including San Mateo County’s, are processing ballots under federal pressure in the final days before Tuesday’s vote.

CalMatters, Oct 29, 2026

Coastal flooding possible along Bay shorelines through Sunday

The National Weather Service warns that high tides could flood low-lying shoreline areas and close some roads around San Francisco Bay and the Pacific coast through Sunday. Friday’s high tide at the San Francisco gauge, about 2:39 p.m., runs nearly 2 feet above normal, easing over the weekend. Light rain is possible Saturday, though most of the Bay Area has only a 10% to 30% chance.

Why it matters: Bayside trails, parking lots and low roads near the shoreline from Millbrae to Foster City can flood around the afternoon high tides.

NBC Bay Area (Bay City News), Oct 30, 2026

Could Prop. 45 open the door to more California data centers?

Prop. 45 would speed environmental reviews for housing, schools and other infrastructure, and some legal experts say its wording on broadband facilities could also let data centers qualify. Supporters, the data center industry and a Legislative Counsel memo say data centers aren’t covered. Backers have raised more than $40 million, while labor and environmental opponents have spent over $31 million.

Why it matters: Peninsula voters decide Prop. 45 on Tuesday, and its reach could affect how much say local governments keep over large projects.

CalMatters, Oct 30, 2026

Sports

Today in sports: Dream force Game 7; Warriors host the Lakers tonight

Atlanta beat the Valkyries 80–69 in Game 6 on Thursday, tying the WNBA Finals 3–3 and setting up a winner-take-all Game 7 Saturday at Chase Center. The Warriors (2–3) host the Lakers tonight in an NBA Cup game after back-to-back road wins. The Earthquakes host Real Salt Lake Saturday at 2 p.m., and the 49ers are on their bye.

ESPN, Oct 29, 2026

Giants start a pivotal off-season with pitching at the top of the list

After a 97-loss season, the Giants need at least two starting pitchers, a closer and a rebuilt bullpen, with little depth behind Logan Webb and Landen Roupp. Buster Posey’s front office must also decide whether to trade any of its big contracts, including Rafael Devers, Willy Adames, Matt Chapman and Jung Hoo Lee. The clock is short: baseball’s labor deal expires Dec. 1, when a lockout and transaction freeze are expected.

NBC Sports Bay Area, Oct 29, 2026

Patrick Stewart, Star Trek’s Captain Picard, dies at 86

Sir Patrick Stewart died Wednesday at his Los Angeles home after a short struggle with Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia, his spokesperson said. He played Jean-Luc Picard across seven seasons of “Star Trek: The Next Generation,” four films and “Star Trek: Picard,” and Professor Charles Xavier in the X-Men films. He was knighted in 2010 and was also known for his one-man “A Christmas Carol.”

Science & tech

Spacewalkers install a new solar array on the 100th U.S. station spacewalk

NASA’s Anil Menon and Canada’s Joshua Kutryk spent 7 hours and 11 minutes outside the International Space Station on Thursday, installing and unrolling one of the last upgraded solar arrays. It was the 100th U.S. spacewalk from the station without a shuttle docked and the first by a Canadian since 2019. The pair is scheduled to install the final array on Nov. 5.

Space.com, Oct 29, 2026

Virgin Galactic’s new Delta-class space plane flies for the first time

Virgin Galactic’s first Delta-class ship, Apeiron, flew attached to its carrier plane from Arizona to Spaceport America in New Mexico. After more tests there, the company plans to start commercial suborbital flights in spring 2027. Nearly 700 ticket holders are waiting, and the latest seats sold for $750,000 each.

Space.com, Oct 30, 2026

Mistral releases the weights of its trillion-parameter Large 4 model

French AI company Mistral made its Mistral Large 4 model free to download on Hugging Face, so businesses and governments can run it on their own systems. Mistral says it matches leading open models while using fewer tokens per task and pitches it for cybersecurity work. It’s also available through Mistral’s API.

TechCrunch, Oct 29, 2026
