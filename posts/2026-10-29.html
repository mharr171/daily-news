Issue No. 29

Thursday, October 29, 2026

A federal judge on Wednesday refused the Trump administration’s request to set aside about 20,000 California ballots, and San Mateo County voters weigh Measure V, an $848 million college bond. Stephen Curry scored 44 in a Warriors win at Minnesota, and the Valkyries can win the WNBA title in Atlanta today. In science and tech, SpaceX finished Starship test fires ahead of Flight 15, and Google rolled out Gemini 4 Carbon to cybersecurity defenders first.

In this edition:

General news

Judge rejects Trump administration bid to set aside 20,000 California ballots

U.S. District Judge David O. Carter on Wednesday turned down the federal government’s request that California election officials segregate about 20,000 ballots from voters it claims may not be citizens, saying states control their own elections. State and county officials say the federal list is riddled with errors, and prosecutors concede some people on it are likely citizens. The administration says it will appeal to the 9th Circuit and possibly the Supreme Court.

Why it matters: Mail ballots already returned by Peninsula voters keep moving through the normal count while the appeal plays out ahead of Tuesday’s election.

CalMatters, Oct 28, 2026

Measure V asks San Mateo County voters to extend an $848 million college bond

The San Mateo County Community College District wants $848 million in bonds for a new gym, arts building upgrades and repairs at Cañada College, College of San Mateo and Skyline College, with about $1.35 billion in total repayment. Critics say few new safeguards have been added since former Chancellor Ron Galatolo was jailed over contracts paid from earlier bonds, and the district’s internal auditor resigned in September. District leaders point to square-footage caps and a whistleblower hotline.

Why it matters: The measure would extend an existing property tax of $18 per $100,000 of assessed value through 2049, about $386 a year on the county’s median-priced home.

Palo Alto Daily Post, Oct 29, 2026

Fed holds rates at 3.75% to 4%, with two officials voting for a hike

The Federal Reserve kept its benchmark rate range at 3.75% to 4% on Wednesday in a 10–2 vote, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack preferring a quarter-point increase. The statement upgraded the economy to “strong” and called inflation elevated, leaving room for a hike. Chair Kevin Warsh also said the Fed will hold fewer rate-setting meetings starting in 2027.

Why it matters: The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5% after the decision, which keeps mortgage and auto-loan rates high for Bay Area buyers.

Wolf Street, Oct 28, 2026

Iran war costs top $47 billion, Pentagon tells Congress

The Pentagon estimates the war with Iran cost $47.1 billion through Oct. 16, with munitions making up more than 60% and stockpiles of some weapons, such as Patriot interceptors, running down. Congressional officials say the figure likely understates the real bill because it leaves out base repairs and other costs. The administration is seeking up to $70 billion in emergency military funding as President Trump holds off on restarting major combat for now.

Why it matters: The spending fight lands in Congress right after Tuesday’s election and affects federal budget priorities that reach California.

The Philadelphia Inquirer (New York Times), Oct 28, 2026

Little-known New York firm emerges as buyer for the empty San Francisco Centre mall

Seven Equity Group, a small New York investor that has quietly bought six distressed office buildings nearby since early 2025, is now trying to buy the vacant San Francisco Centre mall. The 1.2 million-square-foot property is valued at about $130 million, and remaking it could cost hundreds of millions more. Sources say the firm wants to keep retail and add family entertainment.

Why it matters: A buyer for the Market Street mall would be a step toward reviving downtown shopping, a common day trip for Peninsula residents via BART.

The San Francisco Standard, Oct 29, 2026

Stephen Curry scored 44 points as the Warriors won 119–114 at Minnesota on Wednesday for their second straight victory, moving to 2–3; they host the Lakers Friday. Basketball Reference, Oct 28, 2026

Valkyries one win from a title as Game 6 moves to a midday tip in Atlanta

Game 6 of the WNBA Finals between the Valkyries and the Dream will tip off Thursday at 12:30 p.m. Eastern at State Farm Arena, moved from prime time so the Hawks can play there that night. The Dream’s usual home court seats only about 3,500, and the team said in an open letter that its warnings about finding a big enough venue were ignored. A Valkyries win would give Golden State its first championship.

ESPN, Oct 27, 2026

Earthquakes rally for a 2–2 draw with Colorado and stay fifth in the West

Reid Roberts gave San Jose an early lead and Jack Jasinski equalized in the 83rd minute as the Quakes tied the Colorado Rapids 2–2 at PayPal Park on Wednesday. The playoff-bound Quakes sit fifth in the Western Conference with 49 points. They host Real Salt Lake Saturday at 2 p.m. in their final regular-season home game.

San Jose Earthquakes, Oct 28, 2026

Science & tech

SpaceX finishes Starship upper-stage test fires ahead of Flight 15

SpaceX fired the Ship 42 upper stage on the ground Monday and Tuesday, including a full six-engine burn and a single-engine burn that simulates leaving orbit. Elon Musk is targeting Flight 15 for November and says SpaceX will try to catch both the ship and the Super Heavy booster, pending approval. No upper stage has been caught before.

Space.com, Oct 29, 2026

Google’s Gemini 4 Carbon goes first to cybersecurity defenders

Google introduced Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model, with top scores on tests of finding and patching software vulnerabilities. It is going to a small group of cyber defenders and trusted partners first. Google says developers, businesses and everyday users will get it soon, at an introductory price matching Gemini 4 Argon.

Google, Oct 28, 2026

ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers can claim up to $500 a month in API credits

OpenAI now gives eligible ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers up to $500 a month in free API credits as part of their plans. To use them, subscribers link an API organization they own. That lets paying users try building their own tools on OpenAI’s models without a separate bill.
