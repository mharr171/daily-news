General news

Top story

Newsom will commute all 548 California death sentences to life without parole

Gov. Gavin Newsom said Thursday that he will change every death sentence in the state to life in prison without the possibility of parole. The Associated Press reports that the state Supreme Court will have the final say in hundreds of the cases, so the change does not take effect automatically for each inmate. Newsom is term-limited and leaves office in January.

Why it matters: California has the nation’s largest death row, and the decision ends the state’s capital punishment system in practice. It also lands as Newsom is widely discussed as a 2028 presidential contender.

The Associated Press, via CTV News, Nov 5, 2026

San Mateo County counts on: Measure V leads with 64%, and the Millbrae council races stay tight

In KQED’s results as of 8:08 a.m. Wednesday, Measure V, the community college bond that needs 55%, had about 64% yes. In Millbrae, Ann Schneider led Juan Gámez 389 to 374 in the District 3 short-term race, and Albert Yam led Bob Nguyen 535 to 478 in District 4. The regional transit measure was passing in the county with about 62% yes, and the county elections office reported roughly 130,000 ballots still unprocessed as of 4 p.m. Wednesday.

Why it matters: With margins this thin, the two Millbrae seats could change as late ballots are counted, and the county’s official count will take days to finish.

Bay Area Air District votes to advance strict rules for replacing gas water heaters

The Bay Area Air District’s board voted Wednesday to move forward with standards that, starting in 2028, would require households to buy cleaner water heaters when a gas unit wears out, with exemptions for low-income households and complicated installations. The district estimates the rules could prevent up to 85 premature deaths and 15,000 asthma attacks a year. The report comes from the Sierra Club, an advocacy group, which cites an RMI estimate of about $360 a year in savings from switching to a heat pump.

Why it matters: San Mateo County is in the district, so replacing a worn-out gas water heater could mean a different, and possibly costlier, purchase for local homeowners from 2028.

CleanTechnica (Sierra Club release), Nov 4, 2026

A Wisconsin infant dies of measles, the state’s first measles death since 1990

Wisconsin health officials said an infant died from measles. ABC News counts eight reported measles deaths nationally now: six in Pennsylvania, one in New York and one in Wisconsin. The story is still developing.

Why it matters: Measles spreads where vaccination rates slip, and infants too young to be vaccinated are the most exposed, which is why Bay Area health departments keep urging families to check their children’s records.

ABC News, Nov 5, 2026

Good news

Swimmer completes California’s full coastline, about 900 miles in 111 days

Long-distance swimmer Catherine Breed started at the Oregon border on July 1 and finished Oct. 19 at a pier near the Mexican border, swimming past the line more than 3 miles offshore to avoid polluted water near Tijuana. She reported eight shark encounters and rough weather along the way. Good News Network, which cites ABC News for the claim, says she is the first person to swim the whole coast; she had earlier swum from the Golden Gate Bridge to Half Moon Bay.

Good News Network, Nov 4, 2026

Developing

Justice Department opens a criminal investigation into the 2022 Supreme Court abortion-ruling leak

Federal prosecutors in Washington, led by U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro’s office, are investigating who leaked the draft opinion that overturned Roe v. Wade, Fox News reports, after Chief Justice John Roberts’ internal inquiry did not identify the leaker. The Justice Department declined to comment. The New York Times reports the inquiry has accelerated, with subpoenas, and that it takes the administration into the court’s inner workings; critics quoted by the Daily Mail worry it is politically motivated.

Why it matters: A criminal probe of the nation’s highest court raises questions about the separation of powers, and about who at the court could be questioned.

Fox News; The New York Times, Nov 5, 2026

Democrats promise major investigations after winning Congress

The Associated Press reports that corruption topped voters’ concerns as they gave Democrats control of Congress, and that Democratic leaders are now promising major probes of the Trump administration. NPR reports party leaders also face the job of uniting a sometimes fractious caucus around lower costs, the other main promise of their campaigns.

Why it matters: Subpoenas and hearings from a Democratic House will start in January and could shape federal agencies’ actions on issues from health care to immigration that reach California.

The Associated Press, via CTV News; NPR, Nov 5, 2026

Elon Musk spent nearly half a billion dollars on the midterms and has little to show for it

Reuters reports Musk spent almost $500 million, including $445 million on federal races according to OpenSecrets. Of 37 House Republicans he backed, three won and 33 lost, and six of seven Senate candidates he backed lost, with an Alaska race still too close to call. Democrats plan investigations of his companies and a bill from incoming Oversight chair Robert Garcia, and Tesla and SpaceX shares fell 11.4% and 8.5% Thursday afternoon.

Why it matters: Bay Area-linked companies such as Tesla and SpaceX now face a Congress that has said it will scrutinize them.

Voters in five states back citizen-only voting rules

Voters in Kansas, Alaska, Arkansas, South Dakota and West Virginia approved measures barring noncitizens from voting, each with more than 60% support, Fox News reports. Supporters, including the Honest Elections Project, say proof-of-citizenship rules are popular; California voters rejected voter ID Proposition 39 on Tuesday, 58.5% to 41.4%, according to the same report.

Why it matters: It shows how differently states are handling election rules, which matters as California fights federal demands for its voter list.

Democrat Scott Wiener is poised to win the San Francisco seat held by retiring Rep. Nancy Pelosi, The Hill reports, after his opponent, Supervisor Connie Chan, conceded Wednesday evening.

Developing

NASA’s Swift observatory has re-entered the atmosphere over Indonesia

The U.S. Space Force confirmed that Swift re-entered at 7:39 a.m. EST Wednesday over the North Sumatra region of Indonesia, almost 22 years after launch. NASA says most of the spacecraft was expected to burn up, though some components may have survived. Swift launched in 2004 to study gamma-ray bursts and was designed for a two-year prime mission.

Spacewalkers go out to install the space station’s eighth and final solar array

Canadian astronaut Joshua Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon began a spacewalk Thursday to install the last of eight new solar arrays on the station’s starboard truss. It is Kutryk’s second spacewalk in about two weeks; on Oct. 29 the pair installed seven of the eight. Kutryk launched Oct. 1 on SpaceX’s Crew-13 for an expected six-month stay.

Blue Origin pushes New Glenn’s return to flight to January 2027

CEO Dave Limp told staff the rocket will not fly again until January, Bloomberg reports, according to The Next Web. The delay affects 48 Amazon Leo satellites due on the next flight and NASA’s Blue Moon lander plans for Artemis.
