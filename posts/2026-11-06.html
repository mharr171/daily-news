Top story

Meta, Google and TikTok sue California to block its teen social media law

Each company filed its own lawsuit against Attorney General Rob Bonta to stop AB 1709, which Gov. Gavin Newsom signed in September and which takes effect at the start of 2027. The law bars autoplay video and personalized feeds for users under 16 and would require platforms to delete those users’ accounts if the features aren’t switched off, with fines of up to $50,000 per affected user. Meta argues the law violates the First Amendment and is preempted by federal law, and TikTok and Google make similar arguments; Bonta’s office said it is reviewing the filings.

Why it matters: If a court blocks the law, California families would not get the new limits in January, and the case could set a model for other states.

Nithya Raman defeats Mayor Karen Bass in the Los Angeles mayor’s race, CBS News projects

CBS News projects that Councilmember Nithya Raman, a Democratic socialist, will be Los Angeles’ next mayor after beating the incumbent. Raman had advanced past Spencer Pratt in the June primary, and a UC Berkeley IGS and Los Angeles Times poll shortly before the vote had Bass and Raman tied at 40% among likely voters.

Why it matters: Los Angeles is the state’s biggest city, so its next mayor will shape California’s fights over housing, homelessness and public safety that Bay Area leaders face too.

FEMA is behind on approving more than $191 million in Southern California disaster-protection projects

The projects, meant to reduce damage from floods, earthquakes, fires and landslides, are funded through FEMA’s BRIC program, which the administration canceled in March 2025 before a federal judge ordered it continued this spring after California and 19 other states sued. A September GAO report puts the nationwide backlog at $2.2 billion, with California holding the most pending projects of any state. FEMA says it is working to streamline its reviews.

Why it matters: Flood-control and earthquake-retrofit work across California waits on these approvals, and the same federal program serves Bay Area communities.

CBS News Los Angeles, Nov 5, 2026

Good news

Sandwich terns rebound from zero to 756 breeding pairs at an English harbor after avian flu

Breeding pairs of Sandwich terns returned to the Pagham Harbour colony on England’s south coast, climbing from none in the years after avian flu wiped out the earlier population in 2022 to 756 pairs now. Little terns, ringed plovers, black-headed gulls and oystercatchers have also increased. The RSPB and volunteers credit predator-proof fencing, rat monitoring, vegetation clearing and visitor patrols.

U.S. politics & government

Trump declares the Oval Office and Cabinet Room a national monument, and a planning panel approves his arch

Trump signed the proclamation Thursday, his first official action after his party’s midterm losses. It directs the Interior secretary to manage the rooms and requires review before any substantial alteration, but it does not restrict redecorating, and CBS notes a future president could roll it back. Trump said major changes would need Congress’s review, though the proclamation’s text does not say so. Separately, the National Capital Planning Commission voted 8–4 to approve his planned 250-foot triumphal arch, and a federal judge is weighing whether to temporarily block construction.

Why it matters: Taxpayer-funded White House projects, including a nearly $1 billion ballroom that critics cite, are likely targets for the oversight Democrats promise when they take over Congress in January.

Justice Department sues Penn State over in-state tuition for undocumented students

The department argues that Penn State’s lower tuition for undocumented students, while denying the same rate to U.S. citizens from other states, violates federal law, and it asks a court to end the rates. Civil Division head Brett Shumate said Congress made a deliberate policy choice. The department says this is its 27th such suit and that six earlier cases produced orders permanently blocking similar policies; Penn State said it is reviewing the complaint.

Why it matters: California also offers in-state tuition to some undocumented students under state law, so the outcome of these suits could reach the state’s public colleges.

Al Jazeera, Nov 6, 2026

Two Pentagon officials told Fox News that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth signed an Oct. 29 memo allowing up to 60 marked patrol cars, military tactical vehicles, D.C. National Guard boats and up to 20 light towers and generators. Roughly 3,000 Guard troops are deployed in the capital as part of Trump’s crime crackdown.

Why it matters: Federal use of the Guard in cities has been contested in California courts, so changes in how troops operate in Washington will be watched here.

Science & tech

Spacewalkers finish the space station’s solar power upgrade with the eighth and final array

NASA’s Anil Menon and Canada’s Joshua Kutryk installed the last roll-out solar array on the station’s power truss Thursday, a spacewalk of 6 hours 59 minutes. One blanket first failed to produce the expected power, so the crew waited for another night pass and rechecked connections before extending it to its full 63 feet. The original arrays produced about 160 kilowatts, and the final array should raise output to about 215 kilowatts, which Menon called 40% more power. The station is planned for retirement in 2030.
