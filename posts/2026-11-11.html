Top story

Prop. 42, funded largely by Sergey Brin, is narrowly passing and could block the next billionaire tax

Proposition 42, a constitutional amendment that bars new state taxes on personal property such as business stakes, intellectual property and financial assets, was leading with about 51.6% of the vote on Tuesday, with roughly 83% of ballots counted, according to Fortune’s reporting of AP estimates. Its sponsor is funded largely by Brin, who put in $102 million; voters rejected Prop. 40, the one-time billionaire tax, last week. Critics say Prop. 42 could be used to challenge other taxes on capital gains, including Prop. 3, a permanent tax increase on the top 2% of earners that passed.

Why it matters: If it holds, Prop. 42 would limit what California can tax in the future, shaping how the state pays for health care, schools and transit.

What happened to…?

What happened to the fight over closing the National Archives in San Bruno? Court dates are set for early 2027

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued on Oct. 14 to stop the closure of the Bay Area’s only National Archives facility, and a separate suit followed from California Indian Legal Services. The state’s case is assigned to U.S. District Judge Noël Wise with an initial hearing on Feb. 2, 2027, and the tribal legal group’s case to Judge Edward Davila with a hearing on Jan. 28. As of a Nov. 6 update, no one had asked for an emergency order, so the National Archives can keep moving records out until then; it has said the transfers could start as soon as November. An online forum on the closures is set for Friday, Nov. 13.

Why it matters: The San Bruno archive sits next to Millbrae and holds federal immigration, court and family records for Northern California that would move to other states.
