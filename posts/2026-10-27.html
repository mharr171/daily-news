General news

Microsoft offers voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees

Microsoft offered a voluntary exit package to roughly a quarter of its workforce at level 70 and below in the US, Canada, India, and the UK, with each business unit deciding whether to take part. Those who accept leave by year-end with standard severance, and the company said layoffs remain possible.

Why it matters: Microsoft is a major Bay Area employer, and big-tech buyouts ripple through local hiring and the housing market.

The Deep Dive, Oct 27, 2026

King tides flood bayside roads, with more high water due this week

High tides swamped parts of Marin on Monday, flooding a Highway 101 off-ramp and stranding drivers and pedestrians until the water receded. A coastal flood advisory for low-lying shoreline areas runs through Thursday afternoon, with San Francisco’s highest tides about 2 feet above normal around midday today.

Why it matters: Bayfront areas along the Peninsula, including near Millbrae and Burlingame, can see the same midday flooding, so avoid shoreline paths and low roads around high tide.

CBS San Francisco, Oct 26, 2026

California’s last-minute voting guide: mail ballots must be postmarked by Nov. 3

Mail ballots count if they are postmarked by Election Day and arrive within seven days, and early voting centers are open in every county. The ballot carries 14 statewide propositions, including Prop. 40, which would make California the first state with a billionaire tax, plus races for governor and several competitive House seats.

Why it matters: Peninsula voters can still mail a ballot, drop it off, or vote in person at any San Mateo County vote center through Election Day.

CalMatters, Oct 27, 2026

A river otter returns to the San Mateo coast after more than 100 years

Wildlife cameras in a closed area of the Cloverdale Ranch preserve near Pescadero caught a North American river otter this summer, the first documented on the San Mateo coast since the 1800s. Biologists say the otter, which needs clean water and plenty of food, is a sign the creeks there are healthy.

Why it matters: It’s a hopeful sign for the county’s coastal creeks, which also support coho salmon and steelhead.

Star Trek’s Walter Koenig, who played Chekov, dies at 90

Koenig died Sunday at his home in North Hollywood. He played navigator Pavel Chekov in 36 episodes of the original Star Trek and in the first seven films, and later played Alfred Bester on Babylon 5. He also wrote two memoirs about his career.
