Top story

Learjet crashes near Palm Beach, killing all nine aboard

A Learjet 60 flying from Austin toward the Bahamas came down around midday Sunday in a Palm Beach County neighborhood less than seven miles from Mar-a-Lago, killing everyone on board. The plane fell near the entrance to a mobile home park, about 600 residents were moved to shelters, and no injuries on the ground were reported. The FAA and the NTSB are investigating; storms were in the area, but the cause has not been determined.

Why it matters: Business jets are a regular sight at Peninsula airports such as San Carlos and Palo Alto, so the federal findings on what went wrong could matter to pilots and neighbors here.

Housing snapshot

Housing snapshot: San Mateo County’s median home price climbs to $2.33 million

The median price of an existing single-family home sold in San Mateo County in September was $2,327,500, up 8.3% from $2,150,000 a year earlier; the statewide median was $899,720, up 1.7%. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.24% on Oct. 29, down from 7.32% the week before but well above the 6.17% of a year ago. For renters, Apartment List puts the median one-bedroom in neighboring San Francisco at $3,879 and the two-bedroom at $4,597; the citywide median fell 1.0% in October but is up 24.3% over the past year. County-level rent figures weren’t available.

Why it matters: With rates above 7%, the same price tag now means a much bigger monthly payment, which keeps first-time buyers on the Peninsula out of the market and pushes more households toward renting.

California Association of Realtors (September data); Freddie Mac, Oct 29, 2026; Apartment List, Nov 2026

Billionaire tax fight reaches the ballot: Prop. 40 opponents have spent more than $118 million

Proposition 40 would levy a one-time 5% tax on the assets of California residents worth more than $1 billion, with 90% of the money going to health care and 10% to education and food assistance. Fortune reports that a political group funded largely by Google co-founder Sergey Brin, who gave $102 million, has spent about $118.5 million against it and about $186 million for two counter-measures, Props. 41 and 42. The measure is sponsored by a health care workers’ union and backed by Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Ro Khanna.

Why it matters: The state’s nonpartisan analyst says the tax would probably raise tens of billions of dollars over several years, money that would help backfill federal health care cuts that hit Bay Area clinics and patients.

Fortune, Nov 2, 2026

Good news

Five people found alive on a Pacific island after about three months adrift

Villagers on Fanning Island in Kiribati spotted a drifting boat and brought five people ashore on Sunday, including two children. They had set out on July 30 for what was meant to be a one-hour trip in the Federated States of Micronesia, were caught in a storm, and drifted an estimated 3,400 miles; the U.S. Coast Guard ended its search on Aug. 9. Island medical officials have treated them, and four others from their group of nine are still unaccounted for.

Developing

Justice Department asks judge to weigh contempt over analysis of its California voter list

In a Sunday filing, the Justice Department said an immigrant-rights group’s analysis of its list of more than 20,000 suspected noncitizen voters in California violated a judge’s order, and it asked Chief Judge James Boasberg to consider criminal contempt. The group, represented by UCLA’s Voting Rights Project, found that over 40% of the names don’t appear in the state voter file and says it complied with the court’s rules; the department says the list is accurate. Separately, a San Francisco judge ruled Friday that the department can’t demand that election offices remove people from the rolls or set aside their ballots; it appealed Sunday, and a hearing is set for Monday.

Why it matters: California election officials say the list is full of errors and that they can’t finish the legally required removal work before Tuesday, so the court fights could shape how quickly and smoothly results are certified.

Developing

NASA’s Swift observatory has no replacement as it nears reentry

As of Oct. 28 NASA expected the engineless Swift to fall within the next few weeks, but had not published a date, window or location; most of it should burn up, and the risk from debris is described as very small. Swift could swing its telescopes onto a gamma-ray burst within minutes, and NASA has not named a dedicated successor, so the gap could last years, with China’s Einstein Probe and the French-Chinese SVOM mission covering part of the work. Its 22 years of data, about 12 terabytes, stay open to researchers.
