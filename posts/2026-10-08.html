General news

SamTrans marks 50 years of Peninsula bus service from its new Millbrae headquarters

The San Mateo County Transit District began in 1976 by merging city bus lines, and its 1991 purchase of the Peninsula rail corridor led to Caltrain and BART’s extension to SFO. Close to 1,000 people attended an anniversary celebration at the agency’s new Millbrae headquarters, and a spokesperson says ridership fully recovered from the pandemic by March 2025. Rider advocates are pushing for more frequent buses and better stops with shade.

Why it matters: SamTrans is headquartered in Millbrae and runs the buses and on-demand service many county residents use to reach BART, Caltrain and SFO.

Trump revisits his Iran remarks about California cities, saying the U.S. won’t let it happen

At a Texas rally on Wednesday night, President Trump repeated “Let them hit San Diego, let them hit Los Angeles” and then added that the U.S. would not let that happen, in what was billed as a clarification of his Nebraska comments. Gov. Gavin Newsom said Trump was trying to scare voters, and the mayors of both cities again objected. Protesters interrupted the rally several times.

Why it matters: The back-and-forth keeps the Iran war and its fuel costs at the center of the midterm campaign as Californians fill out their ballots.

Wiener and Chan lay out sharply different plans in the race for Pelosi’s seat

In San Francisco’s first open congressional race in nearly four decades, Supervisor Connie Chan, endorsed by Nancy Pelosi, wants a national pause on new AI data centers and backs Prop. 40’s billionaire tax. State Sen. Scott Wiener favors federal AI safety testing and a regulator, would pay cities $10,000 for each new home built, and opposes Prop. 40. Both support moving toward Medicare for All.

Why it matters: The winner will represent the district just north of San Mateo County and shape Bay Area votes on housing, transit and AI in Congress.

ABC7 News, Oct 7, 2026

Too-hot slide delays Flood Park’s new playground again

San Mateo County Parks says the playground’s metal slide topped 130 degrees at peak sun this summer, so the opening is on hold while officials look at adding shade. They hope to open by November, when cooler weather and a lower sun angle should help. The $4.1 million play area in Menlo Park includes a climbing tower, zip lines and a section for ages 2 to 5.

Why it matters: Families across the county have waited since June for the county park’s new playground, and it still has no firm opening date.

Science & tech

NASA and the Energy Department sign a pact to speed nuclear power for the Moon

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and Energy Secretary Chris Wright signed an agreement covering space nuclear power from fuel production through launch and operations, taking effect Nov. 1. It backs a 2028 Space Reactor-1 Freedom mission that leads to a reactor for a future Moon base, with a launch-ready lunar reactor targeted for 2030. The agencies will also supply nuclear heater units for the Dragonfly mission to Titan and ESA’s Mars rover.

NASA, Oct 8, 2026

Crew-12 splashes down in the Pacific off Southern California

The Dragon carrying Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, ESA’s Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos’ Andrey Fedyaev splashed down at 8:34 a.m. Pacific after a deorbit burn about 48 minutes earlier. Recovery teams were securing the capsule and crew. NASA streamed the descent live, including the drogue parachutes opening about four minutes before splashdown.

NASA, Oct 8, 2026

AI voice cloning fuels ‘virtual kidnapping’ calls: how to protect your family

A California mother sent $5,400 over five hours after a caller with a local number claimed cartel members had her adult daughter, using what she believes was an AI-cloned voice; her daughter was safe at work. The FBI calls these virtual kidnappings. Hang up and call your relative directly, agree on a family code word that isn’t online, treat demands for secrecy as a red flag, and report losses at ic3.gov.

1840: Under King Kamehameha III, the Hawaiian Kingdom adopted its first constitution, creating a constitutional monarchy with an elected house of representatives, public schools, and a supreme court.

On this day in science: A melted candy bar leads to the microwave oven

1945: Raytheon filed the first patent for a microwave oven, after engineer Percy Spencer noticed a magnetron had melted the candy bar in his pocket. The first model, the Radarange, weighed more than 750 pounds.

Firsts in history: The first enclosed shopping mall opens

1956: Southdale opened in Edina, Minnesota, as the nation’s first fully enclosed, climate-controlled shopping mall. Designed by architect Victor Gruen, it debuted with 72 tenants.
