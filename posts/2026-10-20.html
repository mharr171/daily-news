Tuesday, October 20, 2026

The 49ers beat Washington 23-17 on Monday night to move to 6-0, while the Valkyries dropped Game 2 of the WNBA Finals. In this edition:

General news

Sequoia district students push back on an all-day phone ban

In a Sequoia Union High School District survey, 94% of 1,267 students opposed a bell-to-bell phone ban, many saying they need to reach parents, while most staff favored one and 62% of parents supported it. Current rules restrict phones during class through the end of this school year but allow them at lunch and between periods. District staff will bring a recommended policy for 2027–28 to the board on Nov. 18.

Why it matters: School phone limits are being debated across Peninsula districts, and Sequoia’s decision will shape daily rules for thousands of San Mateo County teens.

Redwood City Pulse, Oct 19, 2026

California wins a $6 million settlement from 23andMe over its data breach

Attorney General Rob Bonta announced Monday a $6 million settlement with 23andMe over claims it failed to protect customers’ genetic and personal data in a 2023 breach affecting nearly 7 million U.S. users, then misled them about how serious it was. The company, renamed Chrome Holding Co., filed for bankruptcy in 2025, and its assets went to the nonprofit 23andMe Research Institute. Customers can ask the institute to delete their data and samples and withdraw research consent.

Why it matters: Bay Area residents who used 23andMe can request that their genetic data and samples be deleted.

Redwood City Pulse, Oct 20, 2026

Federal prosecutors charge 28 California green card holders with illegal voting

Federal prosecutors charged 28 lawful permanent residents across California, from Los Angeles County to Sacramento, with illegally registering or voting; 22 are accused of voting in at least one election, including the June primary. Attorney General Rob Bonta called the cases tiny against about 23 million registered voters, and Gov. Gavin Newsom called the effort an attempt to suppress voters. It comes days after federal officials sued to block counting of up to 20,000 California ballots.

Why it matters: The charges add pressure on county election offices statewide, including San Mateo County’s, in the final two weeks before Nov. 3.

KQED, Oct 19, 2026

Detained Central Valley farmworkers are being denied lawyers, advocates say

Immigration attorneys say people swept up in a federal enforcement surge in the Central Valley since Oct. 5, which observers estimate has led to as many as 200 arrests, have repeatedly been denied access to lawyers at ICE facilities in Fresno and Bakersfield. Some detainees were quickly moved as far as Texas, and attorneys say dozens are effectively unaccounted for. ICE declined to discuss specific operations.

Why it matters: Bay Area legal aid groups are watching closely amid fears the operation could spread to the region.

KQED, Oct 19, 2026

San Francisco keeps its Flock cameras as other Bay Area cities drop them

Mountain View, Santa Cruz, El Cerrito and other cities have dropped or scaled back Flock license-plate cameras over privacy concerns, but San Francisco is keeping its roughly 400 cameras. Mayor Daniel Lurie cut data retention to 30 days after police disclosed improper searches by outside agencies, and the city will weigh renewing the contract in February 2027.

Why it matters: Peninsula drivers heading into San Francisco pass these cameras, and how long the city keeps plate data affects their privacy too.

KQED, Oct 19, 2026

SF prosecutors charge a construction company over a 2023 trench death

San Francisco prosecutors filed felony involuntary manslaughter charges Monday against D’Arcy & Harty Construction and three of its employees over a 2023 trench collapse in the Lower Haight that killed a 24-year-old worker on a city sewer project. Cal/OSHA had found the trench was not properly supported. A preliminary hearing is set for Nov. 14.

Why it matters: Criminal charges over a workplace death on a city-contracted job are rare and put contractors across the region on notice.

Sports

Today in sports: 49ers move to 6-0, Valkyries’ Finals series is even

The 49ers held off Washington 23-17 on Monday Night Football to stay unbeaten. The Valkyries fell 79-65 to the Atlanta Dream in Game 2 of the WNBA Finals, the Dream’s first Finals win in franchise history, and the series is tied 1-1 with Game 3 on Wednesday at 5 p.m. Pacific. The Warriors open their season Wednesday at the Lakers, and the Earthquakes visit FC Dallas on Saturday.

WNBA, Oct 20, 2026

McCaffrey scores twice as the 49ers hold off Washington to stay perfect

Christian McCaffrey ran for two touchdowns and caught nine passes, and George Kittle scored on an 11-yard catch as the 49ers built a 20-3 halftime lead and held on to beat Washington 23-17 at Levi’s Stadium. Playing without Nick Bosa, Deebo Samuel and Christian Kirk, the defense held Washington to 53 rushing yards and sealed it with a late fourth-down stop and a game-ending sack. Mike Evans had 94 yards on three catches before leaving before halftime; San Francisco (6-0) plays at Atlanta on Sunday at 10 a.m.

It traces his Atlanta upbringing and rise in the music business and details his Bay Area ties, including Too Short buying him and the East Side Boyz out of a bad record deal and his work with E-40 on My Ghetto Report Card.

Science & tech

AI assistant Anna aims to tame working parents’ busy schedules

Anna, an AI personal assistant for working parents built by former Meta and Atlassian product leader Dilani Kahawala, responds to voice prompts, sorts incoming school and family messages, flags calendar conflicts and turns information into tasks. The company says its August public launch spread quickly among parents after months of near-daily beta use.
