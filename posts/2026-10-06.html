Tuesday, October 6, 2026

President Trump suggested Iran could ‘take out’ Los Angeles and San Diego, drawing sharp responses from Gov. Newsom and both cities’ mayors. In this edition:

General news

Trump says Iran could ‘take out’ Los Angeles and San Diego; state leaders push back

At a campaign stop in Grand Island, Nebraska, on Monday, President Trump told the crowd that Iran “can take out a city” and said to let it hit Los Angeles and San Diego, calling the war’s costs a small price to pay. Gov. Gavin Newsom said the president was advocating an attack on more than 10 million Californians, and the mayors of both cities called the remarks dangerous. At the same event Trump signed an order allowing tax-free purchases of dyed diesel as war-driven fuel prices climb.

Why it matters: The Iran war is already driving up gas and diesel prices for Bay Area drivers, and the remarks raise new questions about how Washington views California’s safety.

ABC7 News, Oct 5, 2026

Caltrain weekend ridership runs 50% above pre-pandemic levels

Weekend ridership averaged 21,210 in the 2026 fiscal year, up 40.6% from the year before and about 50% above 2019, after weekend service more than doubled to 66 trains a day with the switch to electric trains. Weekday ridership averaged 40,800, up 37.5%, and South San Francisco and Hayward Park are among the stations now above 2019 weekday levels. Caltrain still warns that without new funding it could close more than a third of its stations and drop weekend service.

Why it matters: Millbrae’s Caltrain and BART station depends on the weekend and frequent service that the agency says is at risk without new funding.

Mountain View Voice (Bay City News), Oct 5, 2026

Tech companies fill empty downtown San Francisco storefronts with branded cafes

Companies like Zoox, which opened a robotaxi rider lounge near Union Square, an AI insurance startup with its own coffee shop, and Capital One are moving into vacant downtown storefronts. Supporters say the spaces bring foot traffic back to empty blocks, while critics say they cater to tech workers more than to everyone else. At least one of the startup cafes has already stalled, with a Dogpatch location closed shortly after opening.

Why it matters: Peninsula commuters heading into the city will see more of these branded spaces as downtown tries to recover its street life.

San Francisco Standard, Oct 5, 2026

Sports

Today in sports: Warriors host the Lakers tonight as 4–0 49ers open as underdogs at Seattle

The Warriors, who lost 104–101 to the Clippers in Honolulu on Sunday, host the Lakers in a preseason game tonight at Chase Center, then visit Portland on Wednesday. The unbeaten 49ers open as about three-point underdogs for Sunday’s game at Seattle, only the second time this season they haven’t been favored. The Earthquakes return Sunday at Colorado.

49ers Webzone, Oct 5, 2026

49ers fullback Kyle Juszczyk expected to miss a few weeks with a knee injury

Juszczyk, a 10-time Pro Bowler, hurt his knee in Sunday’s win over Denver, and ESPN reported he could miss a couple of weeks while the team awaited MRI results. Early indications are that he avoided a long-term injury. Reports point to tight end Brayden Willis as a possible fill-in at fullback for the trip to Seattle.

49ers Webzone, Oct 5, 2026

Thunder’s Jalen Williams breaks his leg as NBA preseason injuries pile up

Oklahoma City forward Jalen Williams fractured his leg and dislocated his ankle and needs surgery, and teammate Cason Wallace has a torn labrum. Orlando’s Jalen Suggs is out about two weeks with a knee bone bruise. Opening weekend also saw Giannis Antetokounmpo debut for Miami, one of several stars, including LeBron James in Philadelphia, playing for new teams this season.

NBA.com, Oct 5, 2026

Culture

Apple TV renews ‘Imperfect Women’ for a second season set on Martha’s Vineyard

The thriller, first billed as a limited series, is coming back with an original story rather than another book adaptation. Kerry Washington returns as Eleanor, who goes back to her hometown in Martha’s Vineyard and uncovers secrets about her past. Apple hasn’t set a premiere date or confirmed whether co-stars Elisabeth Moss and Kate Mara will return.

theGrio, Oct 5, 2026

Tyler Perry’s ‘Doing Life’ is now streaming on Netflix

The drama, which arrived on Netflix Oct. 2, follows a single mother and former prison officer and the ex-convict who once protected her during a riot as they try to rebuild their lives. Naomi Baker and Jay Reeves star. Perry says he drew on real people he has met who kept going through hard circumstances.

theGrio, Oct 6, 2026

Science & tech

NASA sets Crew-12’s return: undocking Wednesday, splashdown off California Thursday

Jessica Meir, Jack Hathaway, ESA’s Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos’ Andrey Fedyaev are scheduled to leave the space station aboard their Dragon at 8:05 a.m. EDT Wednesday, weather permitting. Splashdown is targeted for about 11:34 a.m. EDT Thursday in the Pacific off California, and NASA will stream both. Mission managers are watching weather and recovery-team readiness, so times could shift.

NASA, Oct 5, 2026

New Windows malware uses xAI’s Grok to help it hide on infected PCs

Researchers at Qrator found a malware kit called x47.c that steals passwords, browser cookies and AI account keys, and asks Grok to choose how to stay hidden on each computer. It can also drain prepaid AI credits if it finds an API key. Keep Windows updated, never paste commands a website tells you to run, use unique passwords with two-factor sign-in, and set spending limits on any AI accounts.

Fox News, Oct 5, 2026

On this day

Today in history: “The Jazz Singer” ends the silent era

1927: “The Jazz Singer” premiered and was hailed as the first feature-length talkie, though it had only about two minutes of synchronized dialogue. It was a big hit and helped push Hollywood into sound.

History.com

On this day in science: Astronomers find a planet around a sunlike star

1995: Swiss astronomers Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz announced 51 Pegasi b, the first planet found orbiting a sunlike star. The scorching giant, which circles its star every four days, later earned them a Nobel Prize.

Museum of Science, Boston

Firsts in history: The first U.S. train robbery

1866: Brothers John and Simeon Reno boarded a moving Ohio & Mississippi Railroad train in Jackson County, Indiana, and made off with about $13,000 in what’s considered the first train robbery in U.S. history.
