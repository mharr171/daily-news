In this edition:

General news

San Mateo County declares a local emergency to get ahead of El Niño storms

The Board of Supervisors declared a local emergency on Tuesday so the county’s emergency management office can prepare for a strong-to-very-strong El Niño with fewer bureaucratic hurdles. A new task force will coordinate with cities and agencies through March on risks such as flooding, landslides, coastal erosion, and power outages. Residents are urged to check their flood risk and sign up for SMC Alert, and most cities have opened sandbag sites.

Why it matters: Peninsula cities including Redwood City and Menlo Park flooded in the 2022–23 storms, and early preparation can speed help and supplies this winter.

The Almanac, Oct 21, 2026

Millbrae invites residents to test paid-parking options tonight

Millbrae is drafting a downtown parking management plan and will let residents try two payment systems tonight from 5:30 to 8:30 p.m., one in the Chetcuti Room and one in the library. Rates, hours, zones, and equipment are still undecided and will be set at public City Council meetings. Parking revenue would go into a dedicated fund that can only be spent on parking.

Why it matters: Paid parking would change the cost of shopping, dining, and commuting downtown, and resident feedback can shape the final plan.

City of Millbrae, Oct 22, 2026

Judge limits DHS’s search for noncitizen voters in California and eight other states

Federal Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín ordered the Department of Homeland Security on Wednesday to halt field steps such as interviews when it believes the target could be a U.S. citizen, citing flawed data known to include citizens. The order covers California and eight other states, takes effect Friday afternoon to allow an appeal, and lasts until after the midterms. Voting rights groups argued the effort was scaring citizens away from voting.

Why it matters: The ruling is meant to protect eligible California voters, including naturalized citizens on the Peninsula, from being questioned before Nov. 3.

CNN (via KESQ), Oct 21, 2026

California stages millions of sandbags and criticizes a federal pullback on disasters

During Flood Preparedness Week, Governor Newsom’s office said the state has positioned 6.2 million sandbags, more than 52,000 feet of portable flood barriers, and 400,000 tons of rock at 70 sites ahead of El Niño. The office accused the Trump administration and FEMA of leaving states to handle disasters alone, without citing specific cuts. Residents are urged to use the state’s MyHazards tool to check flood risk at their address.

Why it matters: State supplies and levee work back up local crews if Bay Area storms overwhelm county resources this winter.

Office of the Governor, Oct 21, 2026

Science & tech

NASA lays out when SpaceX and Blue Origin landers should fly to the moon

NASA’s lander program targets Artemis III for the third quarter of 2027, when four astronauts will test docking with both companies’ landers in Earth orbit, ahead of a crewed landing on Artemis IV in early 2028. SpaceX aims for an uncrewed Starship lunar landing demonstration by late 2027, and Blue Origin plans an uncrewed Blue Moon Mark 2 landing in early 2028. NASA scheduled a briefing for Friday on speeding up Artemis.

Space.com, Oct 21, 2026

AI helps uncover decades-old PHP flaws affecting much of the web

The NSA warned Wednesday of two critical flaws in PHP, the language behind a large share of websites, that could let attackers reach a server’s files or database. Researchers at Calif, using OpenAI’s GPT-5.6 Sol, and Google’s Wiz team found the bugs, which date back to PHP 5, and patches are already out. Site owners, including WordPress users, should update to the current PHP version or work with their host.

TechCrunch, Oct 21, 2026

Walmart opens its store to AI shopping agents as Amazon blocks them

Walmart launched Agent Gateway, which lets approved AI assistants search its inventory and buy items for shoppers without seeing their payment details. Starting next month, each agent gets its own profile in a customer’s account, with options to approve every purchase or set a spending limit. The approach contrasts with Amazon, which has begun blocking AI agents from shopping on its site.

TechCrunch, Oct 21, 2026
