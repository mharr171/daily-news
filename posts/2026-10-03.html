Issue No. 3

Saturday, October 3, 2026

The Pentagon is sending a third aircraft carrier group to the Middle East, Brazil heads to the polls Sunday, and President Trump campaigned in Texas for Senate nominee Ken Paxton. The 49ers held Mike Evans and Nick Bosa out of practice ahead of Denver, and the Warriors finish camp in Hawaii today. Victoria Monét’s new album is out, Webb sheds light on planet-building collisions, and Saturn is at its best Sunday night.

In this edition:

General news

Pentagon sends a third carrier group to the Middle East

The U.S. is sending roughly 9,000 more sailors and Marines to the Middle East aboard the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group and the USS Makin Island amphibious group, which could put three carriers in the region by late October. In a Time interview, President Trump said it’s “possible” he will step up bombing of Iran after the Nov. 3 elections.

Why it matters: Wider conflict with Iran could push up oil prices that flow through to Peninsula gas pumps, and Bay Area military families may see loved ones deployed.

Associated Press (KSAT), Oct 1, 2026

Brazil votes Sunday in Lula vs. Flávio Bolsonaro race

Brazilians vote Sunday in a first round pitting President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva against right-wing senator Flávio Bolsonaro, with a Quaest poll showing Lula ahead 39% to 34% but the two tied at 42% in a likely Oct. 25 runoff. Voters also pick all 27 governors, the whole Chamber of Deputies and two-thirds of the Senate.

Why it matters: The result will shape U.S. ties with one of the hemisphere’s largest economies and matters to Bay Area residents with family in Brazil.

MercoPress, Oct 3, 2026

Trump stumps for Ken Paxton at a Texas truck plant

President Trump visited Peterbilt’s plant in Denton, Texas, on Thursday, turning a jobs event into a rally for Republican Senate nominee Ken Paxton, whom he praised for standing by him. He also attacked Paxton’s Democratic opponent, state Rep. James Talarico, as the race heads into its final month.

Why it matters: Control of the U.S. Senate, decided Nov. 3 alongside California’s own races, will shape federal decisions on immigration, disaster aid and transit money that reach San Mateo County.

FOX 7 Austin, Oct 1, 2026

Sports

Today in sports: Warriors wrap Hawaii camp before Sunday’s opener

The Warriors close their training camp at BYU–Hawaii today, with newcomers such as Yaxel Lendeborg getting their first looks, before facing the Clippers on Sunday in Honolulu. The 3-0 49ers host the Broncos on Sunday at 1:25 p.m. on CBS. The Earthquakes are next in action Oct. 10, and the Giants are in their offseason.

NBA.com (Warriors), Oct 2, 2026

Mike Evans and Nick Bosa sit out 49ers practice before Denver game

Receiver Mike Evans (ribs) and defensive end Nick Bosa (knee, calf) didn’t practice Thursday, along with Dre Greenlaw, KhaDarel Hodge, James Thompson and Keion White, as the 49ers prepared for Denver. Left tackle Trent Williams returned on a limited basis after a neck stinger in Week 3, and Evans was seen working on the side; Friday’s report sets game statuses for Sunday.

49ers Webzone, Oct 1, 2026

Culture

Victoria Monét’s Frequency of Love arrives, with her tour ending in San Francisco

Monét’s second studio album, a 22-track set on Lovett Music/RCA, features Bruno Mars, SZA, Bryson Tiller, Leon Thomas, Lucky Daye, Chlöe and her daughter Hazel, and borrows from R&B classics by Marvin Gaye and Tammi Terrell, the Isley Brothers and Brandy. Her Frequency of Love Tour opens Oct. 27 in Raleigh and wraps Dec. 17 in San Francisco.

Rated R&B, Oct 2, 2026

Warner Bros. answers Digger critics with a ‘decide for yourself’ ad

New since Friday’s release: Warner Bros. posted a 27-second spot pairing raves and pans and telling moviegoers to make up their own minds, with Digger hovering around 52% on Rotten Tomatoes. Some trackers projected an opening as low as $7.5 million from about 3,500 theaters, below the studio’s own $12 million to $15 million estimate, and Variety reported the budget grew to $160 million to $180 million.

Forbes, Oct 1, 2026

Science & tech

Webb finds clues to the planet-smashing collisions that build worlds like ours

A team led by Kate Su of the Space Science Institute used Webb and archived Spitzer data to study 21 rare young star systems with unusually warm, dusty disks left by giant collisions. About a third are silica-rich, likely from violent impacts between Mars-sized bodies around stars younger than 300 million years, roughly when Earth and the Moon formed; the rest point to gentler grazing collisions. The study appeared Thursday in The Astrophysical Journal.

NASA, Oct 1, 2026

Anthropic commits $100 million to train 10,000 AI engineers

Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy, pledging $100 million to train 10,000 engineers by the end of 2027 to put its Claude AI to work inside their organizations. The first program pairs an in-person course with a 12-week residency on a real project, with cohorts in San Francisco, New York and London and early participants including Accenture, Deloitte and Morgan Stanley.

Anthropic, Oct 2, 2026

Saturn reaches opposition Sunday, visible all night

Saturn reaches opposition on Sunday, rising around sunset and staying up most of the night, which makes it an easy target this weekend. Later this month, a thin crescent Moon sits near Jupiter before dawn on Oct. 6, and the Orionid meteor shower peaks Oct. 21–22, best seen before dawn after moonset.

NASA, Oct 1, 2026

On this day

Today in history: East and West Germany reunite

1990: Less than a year after the Berlin Wall came down, East and West Germany formally became one country again, a moment many see as the effective end of the Cold War.

History.com

On this day in science: Space shuttle Atlantis makes its maiden flight

1985: Atlantis lifted off for the first time on STS-51J, a classified Defense Department mission. After four days and 64 orbits, it landed at Edwards Air Force Base in California.

NASA

Firsts in history: The first Guinness Book of Records is published

1955: The first edition of the Guinness record book came out in Great Britain, born from a pub-style argument over Europe’s fastest game bird. Compiled by twins Norris and Ross McWhirter, it quickly became a bestseller.
